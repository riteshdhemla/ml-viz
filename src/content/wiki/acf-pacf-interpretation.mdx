# ACF & PACF: Reading the Correlogram

The **correlogram** — the side-by-side plot of the Autocorrelation Function (ACF) and Partial Autocorrelation Function (PACF) — is the single most important diagnostic tool in classical time series analysis. Reading it correctly lets you identify the order of an AR, MA, or ARMA model without fitting anything.

**The questions:**

1. What is the autocorrelation function, and how do you compute it?
2. What is the partial autocorrelation function, and why is it different?
3. How is the PACF computed via the Yule-Walker equations?
4. What are the significance bands on the plots?
5. How do you read AR, MA, and ARMA orders from the patterns?
6. Can we compute the ACF at lags 1–3 by hand?
7. What do seasonal patterns look like in the correlogram?
8. How do you compute all of this in Python?

---

## Q1 — What is the autocorrelation function, and how do you compute it?

Given a weakly stationary series $\{y_t\}$ with mean $\mu$ and variance $\sigma^2$, the **autocovariance at lag $k$** is:

$$
\gamma_k = \text{Cov}(y_t,\, y_{t-k}) = \mathbb{E}[(y_t - \mu)(y_{t-k} - \mu)]
$$

The **autocorrelation at lag $k$** normalises this to the range $[-1, 1]$:

$$
\rho_k = \frac{\gamma_k}{\gamma_0} = \frac{\text{Cov}(y_t, y_{t-k})}{\text{Var}(y_t)}
$$

Note $\rho_0 = 1$ always, and $\rho_k = \rho_{-k}$ (symmetry), so we only plot $k \ge 0$.

**Sample estimate.** Given $T$ observations $y_1, \ldots, y_T$:

$$
\hat{\rho}_k = \frac{\sum_{t=k+1}^{T}(y_t - \bar{y})(y_{t-k} - \bar{y})}{\sum_{t=1}^{T}(y_t - \bar{y})^2}
$$

The ACF captures the **total correlation** between $y_t$ and $y_{t-k}$, including indirect paths through all intermediate lags $y_{t-1}, y_{t-2}, \ldots, y_{t-k+1}$.

---

## Q2 — What is the partial autocorrelation function, and why is it different?

The PACF removes these indirect paths. Formally, the **partial autocorrelation at lag $k$** is the correlation between $y_t$ and $y_{t-k}$ after linearly controlling for $y_{t-1}, y_{t-2}, \ldots, y_{t-k+1}$:

$$
\phi_{kk} = \text{Corr}(y_t - \hat{P}_{k-1}[y_t],\; y_{t-k} - \hat{P}_{k-1}[y_{t-k}])
$$

where $\hat{P}_{k-1}[\cdot]$ denotes the projection onto the linear span of $y_{t-1}, \ldots, y_{t-k+1}$.

---

## Q3 — How is the PACF computed via the Yule-Walker equations?

For a stationary series the PACF values $\phi_{11}, \phi_{22}, \ldots$ can be computed recursively using the **Durbin-Levinson algorithm**, which solves the Yule-Walker system at each lag:

At lag 1:
$$\phi_{11} = \rho_1$$

At lag 2 (Yule-Walker):
$$
\begin{pmatrix} \rho_1 \\ \rho_2 \end{pmatrix}
= \begin{pmatrix} 1 & \rho_1 \\ \rho_1 & 1 \end{pmatrix}
\begin{pmatrix} \phi_{21} \\ \phi_{22} \end{pmatrix}
$$

Solving: $\phi_{22} = (\rho_2 - \rho_1^2) / (1 - \rho_1^2)$.

In general, the $k$-th PACF coefficient $\phi_{kk}$ is the last element of the solution to the $k \times k$ Yule-Walker system:

$$
\boldsymbol{\Gamma}_k \boldsymbol{\phi}_k = \boldsymbol{\rho}_k
$$

where $(\boldsymbol{\Gamma}_k)_{ij} = \rho_{|i-j|}$ is the autocovariance matrix and $\boldsymbol{\rho}_k = [\rho_1, \rho_2, \ldots, \rho_k]^\top$.

---

## Q4 — What are the significance bands on the plots?

Under the **white-noise null hypothesis** ($y_t$ is i.i.d.), all autocorrelations $\rho_k$ for $k > 0$ are zero, and the sample estimates $\hat{\rho}_k$ are approximately normally distributed with standard deviation $1/\sqrt{T}$ for large $T$.

The standard **95% confidence bands** are:

$$
\pm \frac{1.96}{\sqrt{T}}
$$

Any spike outside these bands is considered statistically significant at the 5% level.

**Bartlett's formula** provides a more refined standard error that accounts for the correlation structure when the series is *not* white noise:

$$
\text{SE}(\hat{\rho}_k) \approx \sqrt{\frac{1 + 2\sum_{j=1}^{k-1}\hat{\rho}_j^2}{T}}
$$

Statsmodels uses Bartlett's formula by default in `plot_acf`, which is why the confidence bands widen slightly at larger lags when the series has significant autocorrelation at earlier lags.

## Q5 — How do you read AR, MA, and ARMA orders from the patterns?

This is the core skill. After differencing the series to stationarity, match the ACF/PACF pattern to a model family:

| ACF pattern | PACF pattern | Model suggested |
|-------------|-------------|-----------------|
| Exponential decay (positive or alternating) | Cuts off sharply after lag $p$ | AR($p$) |
| Cuts off sharply after lag $q$ | Exponential decay (positive or alternating) | MA($q$) |
| Tails off gradually (exponential or damped sinusoidal) | Tails off gradually | ARMA($p$, $q$) |
| All spikes within bands at all lags | All spikes within bands at all lags | White noise — model fitted |
| Slow, linear decay — spikes stay significant for many lags | Large spike at lag 1 | Non-stationary — difference again |
| Significant spikes at lags $s, 2s, 3s, \ldots$ (seasonal) | Spike at seasonal lag $s$ | Seasonal component — use SARIMA |

<Callout type="tip" title="Reading 'cuts off' vs 'tails off'">
  **Cuts off** means the ACF (or PACF) drops to zero abruptly at some lag and all subsequent spikes are inside the confidence bands. **Tails off** means the spikes decrease gradually and may remain statistically significant for many lags. The distinction is key: a MA(2) ACF *cuts off* at lag 2; an AR(2) ACF *tails off* with exponentially decaying spikes.
</Callout>

## Q6 — Can we compute the ACF at lags 1–3 by hand?

**Series:** $[1, 3, 2, 4, 3, 5]$, so $T = 6$.

**Step 1 — compute the mean:**
$$\bar{y} = \frac{1 + 3 + 2 + 4 + 3 + 5}{6} = \frac{18}{6} = 3$$

**Step 2 — compute deviations** $d_t = y_t - \bar{y}$:

| $t$ | $y_t$ | $d_t = y_t - 3$ |
|-----|-------|-----------------|
| 1 | 1 | $-2$ |
| 2 | 3 | $0$ |
| 3 | 2 | $-1$ |
| 4 | 4 | $1$ |
| 5 | 3 | $0$ |
| 6 | 5 | $2$ |

**Step 3 — variance** (denominator for all autocorrelations):
$$\gamma_0 = \sum_{t=1}^6 d_t^2 = (-2)^2 + 0^2 + (-1)^2 + 1^2 + 0^2 + 2^2 = 4 + 0 + 1 + 1 + 0 + 4 = 10$$

**Step 4 — autocovariance at lag 1** (sum over pairs $(t, t-1)$ for $t = 2, \ldots, 6$):
$$\gamma_1 = \sum_{t=2}^{6} d_t \cdot d_{t-1} = (0)(-2) + (-1)(0) + (1)(-1) + (0)(1) + (2)(0) = 0 + 0 - 1 + 0 + 0 = -1$$
$$\rho_1 = \frac{-1}{10} = -0.10$$

**Step 5 — autocovariance at lag 2** (pairs $(t, t-2)$ for $t = 3, \ldots, 6$):
$$\gamma_2 = \sum_{t=3}^{6} d_t \cdot d_{t-2} = (-1)(-2) + (1)(0) + (0)(-1) + (2)(1) = 2 + 0 + 0 + 2 = 4$$
$$\rho_2 = \frac{4}{10} = 0.40$$

**Step 6 — autocovariance at lag 3** (pairs $(t, t-3)$ for $t = 4, 5, 6$):
$$\gamma_3 = \sum_{t=4}^{6} d_t \cdot d_{t-3} = (1)(-2) + (0)(0) + (2)(-1) = -2 + 0 - 2 = -4$$
$$\rho_3 = \frac{-4}{10} = -0.40$$

**Summary:**

| Lag $k$ | $\hat{\rho}_k$ |
|---------|---------------|
| 0 | 1.00 |
| 1 | $-0.10$ |
| 2 | $0.40$ |
| 3 | $-0.40$ |

**Significance bands:** $\pm 1.96 / \sqrt{6} \approx \pm 0.80$. All three lags fall inside the bands — for this tiny $T = 6$ sample, none are significant. With more data, the alternating positive-negative pattern might suggest an MA(1) or AR(1) with a negative coefficient.

---

## Q7 — What do seasonal patterns look like in the correlogram?

When the series has a seasonal period $m$ (e.g. $m = 12$ for monthly data), the ACF shows **spikes at multiples of the seasonal lag**: significant $\hat{\rho}_{12}$, $\hat{\rho}_{24}$, $\hat{\rho}_{36}$, and so on. The PACF typically shows a single large spike at lag $m$ with smaller spikes at $2m$.

This pattern survives even after non-seasonal differencing and signals the need for **seasonal differencing** $\Delta_{12} y_t = y_t - y_{t-12}$ and seasonal AR/MA terms in a SARIMA model.

## Q8 — How do you compute all of this in Python?

```python
import numpy as np
import pandas as pd
import matplotlib.pyplot as plt
from statsmodels.tsa.stattools import acf, pacf
from statsmodels.graphics.tsaplots import plot_acf, plot_pacf

# ── Manual ACF computation matching the worked example ────────────────────────
def manual_acf(series, max_lag):
    y = np.array(series, dtype=float)
    T = len(y)
    y_bar = y.mean()
    d = y - y_bar
    gamma_0 = np.sum(d**2)
    rho = [1.0]
    for k in range(1, max_lag + 1):
        gamma_k = np.sum(d[k:] * d[:-k])
        rho.append(gamma_k / gamma_0)
    return np.array(rho)

series_example = [1, 3, 2, 4, 3, 5]
print("Manual ACF lags 0-3:", manual_acf(series_example, 3))
# [ 1.   -0.1   0.4  -0.4]

# ── Statsmodels ACF and PACF ──────────────────────────────────────────────────
np.random.seed(42)
T = 200

# Simulate AR(2): y_t = 0.6 y_{t-1} - 0.3 y_{t-2} + eps
eps = np.random.normal(0, 1, T)
y_ar2 = np.zeros(T)
for t in range(2, T):
    y_ar2[t] = 0.6 * y_ar2[t-1] - 0.3 * y_ar2[t-2] + eps[t]

fig, axes = plt.subplots(1, 2, figsize=(12, 4))
plot_acf( y_ar2, lags=20, ax=axes[0], title="AR(2) — ACF (should tail off)")
plot_pacf(y_ar2, lags=20, ax=axes[1], title="AR(2) — PACF (should cut off at lag 2)")
plt.tight_layout()
plt.show()

# Simulate MA(2): y_t = eps_t + 0.5 eps_{t-1} - 0.4 eps_{t-2}
eps = np.random.normal(0, 1, T)
y_ma2 = eps.copy()
for t in range(2, T):
    y_ma2[t] = eps[t] + 0.5 * eps[t-1] - 0.4 * eps[t-2]

fig, axes = plt.subplots(1, 2, figsize=(12, 4))
plot_acf( y_ma2, lags=20, ax=axes[0], title="MA(2) — ACF (should cut off at lag 2)")
plot_pacf(y_ma2, lags=20, ax=axes[1], title="MA(2) — PACF (should tail off)")
plt.tight_layout()
plt.show()

# ── Raw ACF/PACF values ───────────────────────────────────────────────────────
acf_vals  = acf(y_ar2,  nlags=10)
pacf_vals = pacf(y_ar2, nlags=10)
bands = 1.96 / np.sqrt(T)

print("\nAR(2) ACF at lags 0-5:", np.round(acf_vals[:6], 3))
print("AR(2) PACF at lags 0-5:", np.round(pacf_vals[:6], 3))
print(f"95% bands: ±{bands:.3f}")
```
