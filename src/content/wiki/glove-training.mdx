# GloVe Training Procedure

[Word2Vec](/courses/nlp/02-word-embeddings) uses **local context windows**. GloVe (Pennington et al., 2014) instead trains on **global co-occurrence statistics**: it factorizes a corpus-wide count matrix rather than streaming over windows.

**The questions:**

1. What is the key insight — why do co-occurrence ratios carry meaning?
2. What is the full training procedure?
3. Why train on log counts instead of raw counts?
4. What does the weighting function do on a concrete numeric example?
5. How does GloVe compare to Word2Vec?

---

## Q1 — What is the key insight, and why do co-occurrence ratios carry meaning?

The ratio $P(k \mid \text{ice})\, /\, P(k \mid \text{steam})$ is informative about word relationships:

| Probe word $k$ | $P(k \mid \text{ice})$ | $P(k \mid \text{steam})$ | Ratio |
|---|---|---|---|
| solid | high | low | **large** |
| gas | low | high | **small** |
| water | high | high | ≈ 1 |
| fashion | low | low | ≈ 1 |

GloVe is designed so that *differences of word vectors* capture these ratios — which is exactly what makes analogies ("king − man + woman ≈ queen") work.

---

## Q2 — What is the full training procedure?

1. **Build the co-occurrence matrix** $X \in \mathbb{R}^{V \times V}$ over the corpus: $X_{ij}$ counts how often word $j$ appears within a context window of word $i$ (typical window size: 10 words, symmetric). One pass over the corpus; the matrix is sparse.

2. **Downweight frequent pairs** with a capping function:

$$
f(X_{ij}) = \min\!\left(1,\; (X_{ij}/x_\text{max})^{0.75}\right)
$$

   (common choice: $x_\text{max} = 100$). This stops extremely common pairs ("the" co-occurring with everything) from dominating the loss, while ignoring pairs that never co-occur ($f(0) = 0$ — those terms vanish entirely).

3. **Learn two embedding matrices** $\mathbf{V}$ (main) and $\tilde{\mathbf{V}}$ (context) plus bias vectors by minimizing the weighted least-squares objective:

$$
\mathcal{L} = \sum_{i,j=1}^V f(X_{ij})\left(\mathbf{v}_i^\top \tilde{\mathbf{v}}_j + b_i + \tilde{b}_j - \log X_{ij}\right)^2
$$

   Each term pushes the dot product of two word vectors toward the **log co-occurrence count** — frequent companions get aligned vectors.

4. **Final embedding** = $\mathbf{v}_i + \tilde{\mathbf{v}}_i$ (sum of both matrices for each word). The two matrices are symmetric in the objective, and summing them gives a small but consistent quality boost.

---

## Q3 — Why train on log counts instead of raw counts?

The objective in Q2 targets the **log** of each co-occurrence count rather than the raw count:

<Callout type="tip" title="Why log counts?">
  Co-occurrence counts span many orders of magnitude (Zipf's law). Taking the log compresses this range so the least-squares objective isn't dominated by the handful of extremely frequent pairs — and it makes vector *differences* correspond to count *ratios*: $\mathbf{v}_a^\top\tilde{\mathbf{v}}_k - \mathbf{v}_b^\top\tilde{\mathbf{v}}_k \approx \log\frac{X_{ak}}{X_{bk}}$.
</Callout>

## Q4 — What does the weighting function do on a concrete numeric example?

Two pairs, $x_\text{max}=100$:

**Frequent pair** — "ice" and "solid" co-occur $X_{ij}=100$ times:

$$
f(100) = \min\!\bigl(1,\,(100/100)^{0.75}\bigr) = 1.0, \quad \log X_{ij} = \log 100 \approx 4.605
$$

If the current dot product $\mathbf{v}_i^\top\tilde{\mathbf{v}}_j + b_i + \tilde{b}_j = 4.0$, this pair contributes $(4.0 - 4.605)^2 \approx 0.366$ to the loss (weight 1.0, full penalty).

**Rare pair** — "ice" and "fashion" co-occur $X_{ij}=1$ time:

$$
f(1) = (1/100)^{0.75} = 0.01^{0.75} \approx 0.032, \quad \log X_{ij} = 0
$$

Even if the predicted dot product is off by 1.0, this pair contributes only $0.032 \times 1.0 \approx 0.032$ — roughly 11× less than the frequent pair. The weighting function deliberately deprioritizes rare co-occurrences whose counts are noisy.

---

## Q5 — How does GloVe compare to Word2Vec?

| Property | Word2Vec | GloVe |
|----------|----------|-------|
| Training objective | Local context window | Global co-occurrence matrix |
| Computation | Sequential (online SGD) | Can be parallelized |
| Performance | Similar | Similar |
| Transfer | Good | Good |

In practice the two produce embeddings of comparable quality; GloVe's batch formulation parallelizes more naturally, while Word2Vec streams over arbitrarily large corpora without materializing a matrix.
