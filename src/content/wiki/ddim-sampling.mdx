# DDIM Sampling

Standard DDPM sampling requires walking back through all $T = 1000$ noise levels — one network call per step. **DDIM** (Denoising Diffusion Implicit Models, Song et al., 2021) reformulates the reverse process as a deterministic ODE, which makes **step-skipping** valid: you can visit 50 of the 1000 timesteps and still land on a high-quality sample.

## Why step-skipping is valid

DDPM's reverse process is stochastic — each step adds fresh noise, so the trajectory is a random walk that must be taken one step at a time. DDIM removes the stochasticity: given the model's noise prediction, both the "clean image estimate" and the next point on the trajectory are *deterministic functions* of the current point. A deterministic trajectory can be integrated with large steps, exactly like solving an ODE with a coarser grid.

## The DDIM sampling procedure

Recall the cumulative noise coefficient $\bar{\alpha}_t = \prod_{s=1}^{t}(1-\beta_s)$, where $\beta_s$ is the noise variance added at step $s$. Because $\beta_s$ is small, $\bar{\alpha}_t$ decreases from $\approx 1$ (clean) to $\approx 0$ (pure noise) as $t$ increases from $0$ to $T$.

1. Choose a subsequence $\tau = [\tau_1 > \tau_2 > \cdots > \tau_S]$ of timesteps to visit (e.g. every 20th step for $S=50$).
2. Start from pure noise $\mathbf{x}_{\tau_1} \sim \mathcal{N}(\mathbf{0}, \mathbf{I})$.
3. At each step $s$ (going from $\tau_s$ down to $\tau_{s+1}$):
   - Run the network to predict the noise: $\hat{\boldsymbol{\epsilon}} = \boldsymbol{\epsilon}_\theta(\mathbf{x}_{\tau_s}, \tau_s)$.
   - Estimate the clean image:

$$
\hat{\mathbf{x}}_0 = \frac{\mathbf{x}_{\tau_s} - \sqrt{1-\bar{\alpha}_{\tau_s}}\,\hat{\boldsymbol{\epsilon}}}{\sqrt{\bar{\alpha}_{\tau_s}}}
$$

   - Step to the next timestep by re-noising the estimate to the target noise level:

$$
\mathbf{x}_{\tau_{s+1}} = \sqrt{\bar{\alpha}_{\tau_{s+1}}}\,\hat{\mathbf{x}}_0 + \sqrt{1 - \bar{\alpha}_{\tau_{s+1}}}\,\hat{\boldsymbol{\epsilon}}
$$

4. Return $\hat{\mathbf{x}}_0$ at the final step.

Read step 3 as: *guess the clean image, then re-noise that guess to exactly the next (lower) noise level* — using the same predicted noise direction rather than a fresh random draw.

**What to notice:**

- **Frame 2 — the first guess is worthless, and that is fine.** At $t=999$ the clean-image estimate $\hat{x}_0$ sits 1.35 from the nearest mode with responsibilities spread across all three. From pure noise the best possible estimate is near the distribution's mean. What makes the step work is not the guess's quality but that it is re-noised using the *same* $\hat\epsilon$ rather than a fresh draw.
- **Frame 3 — follow $\hat{x}_0$, not $x$.** The running guess migrates from the distribution mean onto one mode as responsibilities collapse (1.35 → 0.08, max responsibility 0.49 → 1.00). The commitment happens early: by the halfway point the outcome is settled.
- **Frame 5 — the speedup claim, measured.** Against a 1000-step reference from the *same* starting noise, endpoint error falls 1.651 at 2 steps to 0.027 at 50, monotonically. That is the ODE argument working: a deterministic trajectory integrated on a coarser grid.

The trace also measures what $\eta$ costs, and it is not sample quality. With a perfect $\epsilon$ both samplers target the right distribution; what $\eta > 0$ destroys is that the endpoint is a *function* of the start. Two $\eta = 1$ runs from an identical starting noise land 2.14 apart at 50 steps. Semantic interpolation and reproducible seeds both depend on $\eta = 0$.

## Properties of DDIM

- **~20× speedup** — reducing $T=1000$ to $S=50$ steps loses little quality.
- **Deterministic** — the same starting noise $\mathbf{x}_{\tau_1}$ always produces the same image. This enables **semantic interpolation**: interpolate between two starting noises and the outputs interpolate smoothly.
- **Same trained model** — DDIM is a *sampling* procedure; it reuses a standard DDPM-trained noise predictor with no retraining.

<Callout type="info" title="The η knob">
  The full DDIM formulation has a parameter $\eta \in [0, 1]$ interpolating between deterministic DDIM ($\eta = 0$, shown above) and stochastic DDPM ($\eta = 1$). Modern samplers (DPM-Solver, UniPC) push the same ODE view further with higher-order solvers, reaching good samples in 10–20 steps.
</Callout>
