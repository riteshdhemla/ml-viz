# Design a Music & Audio Generation System

<SystemDesignMeta
  track="Generative AI System Design"
  company="Suno / ElevenLabs"
  scale="minutes-long coherent audio · streaming · copyright-sensitive"
  difficulty="advanced"
/>

Text-to-music/audio generation shares the generative-AI playbook but has its own
twists: audio is a **very long, high-sample-rate signal**, music needs **long-range
structure** (a song has verses and choruses, not just local coherence), and
**copyright** is uniquely fraught (styles, voices, melodies). The dominant trick is
generating in a **compressed audio-token / latent space**, not raw waveform.

## 1. Clarify the problem

- **Goal:** generate music or audio (song, sound effect, voice) from a text
  prompt (and optionally lyrics/reference), coherent over minutes.
- **The hard axes:** **long-range structure** (musical form) and **high temporal
  resolution** (audio is tens of thousands of samples/second).
- **Copyright:** generating in the style/voice of real artists is the defining
  legal risk.
- **Scope:** the generation pipeline (model + serving + safety).

<Callout type="tip" title="Interview signal">
Raw waveforms are far too long to model directly — name the **neural audio codec /
tokenization** (compress audio into a short token sequence) as the enabling move,
plus **long-range musical structure** as the quality challenge.
</Callout>

## 2. Requirements & scale

| Dimension | Target | Consequence |
|---|---|---|
| Duration | Minutes of coherent audio | Long-range modeling; hierarchical generation |
| Resolution | High sample rate | Model compressed tokens/latents, not raw samples |
| Latency | Streamable | Chunked/streaming synthesis |
| Copyright | No artist/voice cloning without consent | Style/voice filters, provenance |

## 3. GenAI problem framing

Two dominant families:

- **Tokenized-audio autoregressive:** a **neural audio codec** (e.g. residual
  vector quantization) compresses audio into discrete tokens; a transformer
  models the token sequence (MusicGen/AudioLM-style). Long context handles
  structure.
- **Latent diffusion on audio** (or mel-spectrogram): diffuse in a compressed
  audio latent, then decode to waveform.

*Pre-generative baseline?* Sample libraries + rule-based composition — no
open-ended text control. Generative models turn a prompt into novel audio.

## 4. Data & training *(spine: data)*

- **Data:** licensed music/audio with text/metadata; captioning and
  genre/instrument tagging are subsystems.
- **Representation:** train the **audio codec** first (compress → discrete
  tokens/latents), then the generative model over that compact space.
- **Copyright hygiene:** licensing provenance and artist opt-outs are central, not
  an afterthought.

## 5. Model & generation *(spine: hypothesis-space)*

- **Codec:** RVQ-based neural codec turns waveform into a short sequence of tokens
  (the key to tractable length).
- **Generator:** a transformer (autoregressive over tokens) or latent diffusion,
  conditioned on text (+ lyrics/melody); **hierarchical** coarse→fine token
  generation captures both structure and detail.
- **Vocoder/decoder:** tokens/latents → waveform.

<DiffusionViz />

Whether autoregressive or diffusion, the win is the same as images/video:
**generate in a compressed representation**, then decode — never model raw signal
directly.

## 6. Serving & scaling

Generation is a GPU job; support **streaming** by generating and decoding in
chunks so playback can start before the full track is done. Cache the codec/
vocoder; batch generation requests; store outputs in object storage + CDN. Voice
synthesis (TTS) is lower-latency and can be near-real-time with a light vocoder.

## 7. Evaluation *(spine: evaluation)*

- **Automated:** Fréchet Audio Distance (FAD), prompt–audio alignment (CLAP
  score), and objective quality proxies.
- **Human eval** dominates: musicality, structure, prompt adherence, and (for
  voice) naturalness (MOS).
- **Structure checks:** does a 2-minute song hold together, or drift/repeat?

## 8. Safety & guardrails

- **Voice cloning:** synthesizing a real person's voice without consent is the top
  abuse — consent gates, voice-likeness detection, watermarking.
- **Style/copyright:** filter prompts targeting specific artists; monitor for
  melodic/verbatim regurgitation of training tracks.
- **Provenance:** watermark generated audio (audible/inaudible + metadata).

## 9. Tradeoffs & alternatives

| Choice | Alternative | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Tokenized/latent audio | Raw waveform modeling | Waveforms are far too long to model directly |
| Hierarchical coarse→fine | Flat single-scale generation | Captures both long structure and fine detail |
| Streaming chunked synthesis | Generate-then-serve whole | Lets playback start quickly |
| Licensed data + opt-outs | Scrape everything | Copyright exposure is existential here |

## 10. Interviewer follow-ups

<Details summary="Why not model the raw audio waveform directly?">
A few seconds of audio is tens of thousands of samples — sequence lengths are
intractable and local sample prediction misses musical structure. A neural codec
compresses audio into a short token/latent sequence, so the generative model
works over a tractable representation and a decoder reconstructs the waveform.
</Details>

<Details summary="A 10-second clip sounds great but a 3-minute song falls apart. Why?">
Long-range structure. Local coherence is easy; maintaining verses/choruses and
key over minutes needs long context and hierarchical (coarse structure → fine
detail) generation, plus conditioning that encodes song form. Evaluate structure
explicitly, not just short-clip quality.
</Details>

<Details summary="A user prompts 'a song in the voice of [famous artist].' What happens?">
Blocked: prompt filters catch targeted artist/voice cloning, voice-likeness
detection gates outputs, and provenance watermarking marks anything generated.
Unconsented voice/style cloning is the defining copyright and likeness risk for
audio, so it's a hard guardrail backed by licensing hygiene in training.
</Details>

## Key takeaways

- Audio/music generation models a **compressed audio representation** (neural
  codec tokens or latents), never raw waveform.
- **Long-range structure** (via hierarchical generation) is the quality challenge;
  **streaming chunked synthesis** enables fast playback.
- **Voice cloning and style copyright** are the defining safety risks — consent
  gates, likeness detection, and watermarking are mandatory.
