# Design a Music & Audio Generation System

<SystemDesignMeta
  track="Generative AI System Design"
  company="Suno / ElevenLabs"
  scale="minutes-long coherent audio · streaming · copyright-sensitive"
  difficulty="advanced"
/>

Text-to-music/audio generation shares the generative-AI playbook but has its own
twists: audio is a **very long, high-sample-rate signal**, music needs **long-range
structure** (a song has verses and choruses, not just local coherence), and
**copyright** is uniquely fraught (styles, voices, melodies). The dominant trick is
generating in a **compressed audio-token / latent space**, not raw waveform.

## 1. Clarify the problem

- **Goal:** generate music or audio (song, sound effect, voice) from a text
  prompt (and optionally lyrics/reference), coherent over minutes.
- **The hard axes:** **long-range structure** (musical form) and **high temporal
  resolution** (audio is tens of thousands of samples/second).
- **Copyright:** generating in the style/voice of real artists is the defining
  legal risk.
- **Scope:** the generation pipeline (model + serving + safety).

<Callout type="tip" title="Interview signal">
Raw waveforms are far too long to model directly — name the **neural audio codec /
tokenization** (compress audio into a short token sequence) as the enabling move,
plus **long-range musical structure** as the quality challenge.
</Callout>

<ThinkFirst question="A 3-minute song at 44.1kHz stereo — roughly how many samples is that, and what does the number rule out?">
About **16 million samples** (2 × 44,100 × 180). No sequence model attends
over that directly — raw-waveform generation is off the table. The enabling
move is a **neural audio codec**: compress audio into a short discrete-token
or latent sequence (tens to hundreds of steps per second, not tens of
thousands of samples), generate in that space, and decode back to waveform.
</ThinkFirst>

## 2. Requirements & scale

| Dimension | Target | Consequence |
|---|---|---|
| Duration | Minutes of coherent audio | Long-range modeling; hierarchical generation |
| Resolution | High sample rate | Model compressed tokens/latents, not raw samples |
| Latency | Streamable | Chunked/streaming synthesis |
| Copyright | No artist/voice cloning without consent | Style/voice filters, provenance |

**Back-of-envelope on compression:** EnCodec-style codecs turn 32kHz music into
~50 token-steps per second via residual vector quantization — MusicGen models
4 parallel codebooks at that rate, so a 3-minute song is ~9K steps instead of
16M samples ([Copet et al., 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.05284)). That
~1000× reduction is the entire feasibility argument.

## 3. GenAI problem framing

Two dominant families:

- **Tokenized-audio autoregressive:** a **neural audio codec** with residual
  vector quantization compresses audio into discrete tokens
  ([Défossez et al., 2022](https://arxiv.org/abs/2210.13438)); a transformer
  models the token sequence. AudioLM's key split: **semantic tokens** carry
  long-term structure, **acoustic tokens** carry fidelity, generated
  coarse-to-fine ([Borsos et al., 2022](https://arxiv.org/abs/2209.03143)).
  MusicGen collapses this to a single-stage LM with interleaved codebooks.
- **Latent diffusion on audio:** diffuse in a compressed audio latent, then
  decode — Stable Audio's 907M-parameter latent diffusion model with timing
  conditioning generates variable-length stereo 44.1kHz music
  ([Evans et al., 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.04825)).

*Pre-generative baseline?* Sample libraries + rule-based composition — no
open-ended text control. Generative models turn a prompt into novel audio.

<ThinkFirst question="Which do you train first — the generator or the codec? And why does codec quality put a hard ceiling on the whole product?">
**Codec first.** The codec defines the vocabulary the generator writes in:
every generation is decoded through it, so the product can never sound better
than the codec's reconstructions — artifacts in the decoder appear in every
output, forever. That's why codecs are trained separately with reconstruction
plus adversarial (spectrogram-discriminator) losses before any generative
training, and why a codec upgrade is a whole-catalog quality upgrade.
</ThinkFirst>

## 4. Data & training *(spine: data)*

- **Data:** licensed music/audio with text/metadata; captioning and
  genre/instrument tagging are subsystems (MusicLM released MusicCaps — 5.5K
  expert-written music descriptions, because such data barely existed).
- **Representation:** train the **audio codec** first (compress → discrete
  tokens/latents), then the generative model over that compact space.
- **Copyright hygiene:** licensing provenance and artist opt-outs are central,
  not an afterthought — Stable Audio trained on ~19.5K hours of *licensed*
  stock-library audio rather than scraped music, a deliberately smaller but
  litigable-risk-free corpus.

<ThinkFirst question="Your model produces gorgeous 10-second clips, but 3-minute songs meander and repeat. What's missing?">
**Long-range structure.** Local coherence (the next bar sounds right) is a
different skill from musical form (verses return, a chorus repeats, the key
resolves). The fix is **hierarchical generation** — model coarse
structural/semantic tokens first, then fill in fine acoustic detail
conditioned on them — plus long context and explicit form conditioning
(lyrics, section tags). This is exactly AudioLM/MusicLM's semantic→acoustic
split.
</ThinkFirst>

## 5. Model & generation *(spine: hypothesis-space)*

- **Codec:** RVQ-based neural codec turns waveform into a short multi-codebook
  token sequence (the key to tractable length).
- **Generator:** a transformer (autoregressive over tokens) or latent diffusion,
  conditioned on text (+ lyrics/melody). **Hierarchical coarse→fine** generation
  captures both structure and detail — MusicLM chains MuLan text embeddings →
  semantic tokens → acoustic tokens and stays consistent over minutes at 24kHz
  ([Agostinelli et al., 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.11325)).
- **Vocoder/decoder:** tokens/latents → waveform.

<DiffusionViz />

Whether autoregressive or diffusion, the win is the same as images/video:
**generate in a compressed representation**, then decode — never model raw signal
directly.

<ThinkFirst question="Two products: a sound-effects tool that should respond in about a second, and a full-song generator where users happily wait a minute. Map each to the two model families — what's the time-to-first-audio of each?">
Autoregressive token models decode **causally**, so you can stream: decode
the first chunk of tokens, vocode, start playback while generation continues
— time-to-first-audio is sub-second even if the full track takes a minute.
Diffusion denoises the **whole window jointly** — Stable Audio renders 95
seconds in ~8 seconds on an A100, far faster than real time but nothing is
playable until the last denoising step. So: diffusion suits short
effects/stems and batch song rendering; AR tokens suit streaming playback
and interactive voice.
</ThinkFirst>

## 6. Serving & scaling

Generation is a GPU job; support **streaming** by generating and decoding in
chunks so playback starts before the full track is done (AR models stream
natively; diffusion models render segments). Cache the codec/vocoder; batch
generation requests; store outputs in object storage + CDN. Voice synthesis
(TTS) is the latency-critical sibling — light causal vocoders make it
near-real-time.

<ThinkFirst question="No clicks, no ground truth, and taste is subjective. A new checkpoint lands — how do you decide it's actually better?">
Screen automatically, decide with humans. FAD and CLAP-style text–audio
alignment on a fixed prompt suite (e.g. MusicCaps) catch regressions cheaply;
then **pairwise human preference** on musicality, structure, and prompt
adherence makes the call — MusicLM's evaluation used exactly this
metric-plus-listener protocol, and for voice, MOS naturalness panels. The
structural failure mode (songs that drift over minutes) only shows up if you
evaluate *full-length* outputs, so don't A/B on 10-second clips.
</ThinkFirst>

## 7. Evaluation *(spine: evaluation)*

- **Automated:** Fréchet Audio Distance (FAD), prompt–audio alignment (CLAP
  score), and objective quality proxies.
- **Human eval** dominates: musicality, structure, prompt adherence, and (for
  voice) naturalness (MOS).
- **Structure checks:** does a 2-minute song hold together, or drift/repeat?
  Evaluate at full product length, not clip length.
- **Regurgitation audits:** search generations against the training catalog
  (melody/audio fingerprinting) for memorized material.

<ThinkFirst question="What's the abuse case unique to audio generation — the one that's worse here than in any other modality?">
**Voice cloning.** A convincing synthetic voice enables fraud (fake calls from
a family member or CEO), nonconsensual impersonation, and misinformation — and
audio deepfakes are cheap to make and hard for humans to spot. That's why
consent gates on voice reference, voice-likeness detection on outputs, and
watermarking are hard requirements, ahead of general content filtering.
</ThinkFirst>

## 8. Safety & guardrails

- **Voice cloning:** synthesizing a real person's voice without consent is the
  top abuse — consent gates, voice-likeness detection, watermarking. Meta's
  AudioSeal embeds a watermark detectable at the *sample level* with a
  single-pass detector fast enough for real-time screening
  ([San Roman et al., 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.17264)).
- **Style/copyright:** filter prompts targeting specific artists; monitor for
  melodic/verbatim regurgitation of training tracks via fingerprinting.
- **Provenance:** watermark generated audio (inaudible + signed metadata).

## 9. Tradeoffs & alternatives

| Choice | Alternative | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Tokenized/latent audio | Raw waveform modeling | ~1000× shorter sequences; waveforms are intractable |
| Hierarchical coarse→fine | Flat single-scale generation | Captures both long structure and fine detail |
| AR tokens (streamable) | Latent diffusion (whole-window) | Sub-second first audio vs. faster batch rendering |
| Licensed data + opt-outs | Scrape everything | Copyright exposure is existential here |

## 10. Interviewer follow-ups

<Details summary="Why not model the raw audio waveform directly?">
A 3-minute song is ~16M samples — sequence lengths are intractable and local
sample prediction misses musical structure. An RVQ neural codec compresses
audio ~1000× into a short token/latent sequence, so the generative model works
over a tractable representation and a decoder reconstructs the waveform. The
codec's reconstruction quality is then a hard ceiling on output quality.
</Details>

<Details summary="A 10-second clip sounds great but a 3-minute song falls apart. Why?">
Long-range structure. Local coherence is easy; maintaining verses/choruses and
key over minutes needs long context and hierarchical (semantic/structural
tokens → acoustic detail) generation, plus conditioning that encodes song form
(lyrics, section tags). Evaluate structure explicitly at full length, not just
short-clip quality.
</Details>

<Details summary="A user prompts 'a song in the voice of [famous artist].' What happens?">
Blocked: prompt filters catch targeted artist/voice cloning, voice-likeness
detection gates outputs, and provenance watermarking marks anything generated.
Unconsented voice/style cloning is the defining copyright and likeness risk for
audio, so it's a hard guardrail backed by licensing hygiene in training.
</Details>

## Key takeaways

- Audio/music generation models a **compressed audio representation** (RVQ codec
  tokens or latents, ~1000× shorter than waveform), and codec quality caps the
  product.
- **Long-range structure** (via hierarchical semantic→acoustic generation) is
  the quality challenge; **AR tokens stream, diffusion batch-renders** — pick
  per product.
- **Voice cloning and style copyright** are the defining safety risks — consent
  gates, likeness detection, licensed data, and watermarking are mandatory.
