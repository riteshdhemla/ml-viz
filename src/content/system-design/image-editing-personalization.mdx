# Design an Image Editing & Personalization System

<SystemDesignMeta
  track="Generative AI System Design"
  company="Adobe Firefly / DreamBooth"
  scale="mask-conditioned edits · per-user personalization"
  difficulty="advanced"
/>

Beyond "generate from a prompt," a real product needs to **edit** existing images
(inpaint a region, extend a canvas, change a style) and **personalize** to a
specific subject ("put *my dog* in a spacesuit"). This adds **conditioning on an
input image + mask** and **lightweight per-user fine-tuning** on top of a diffusion
base — the interesting system-design twists over plain text-to-image.

## 1. Clarify the problem

- **Goal:** two capabilities — (a) **editing**: inpainting/outpainting/instructed
  edits that change only what's asked while keeping the rest coherent; (b)
  **personalization**: learning a new subject/style from a few user images and
  generating it in new contexts.
- **Quality bar:** edits blend seamlessly (no seams); personalization preserves
  **identity** without overfitting.
- **Scope:** conditioning + personalization on a diffusion base + serving/safety.
  Assumes the base text-to-image case as foundation.

<Callout type="tip" title="Interview signal">
The two hard subproblems are **mask-conditioned generation that stays coherent**
and **fast, cheap per-user personalization**. Naming personalization as a
*fine-tuning* problem (not prompting) is the key fork.
</Callout>

## 2. Requirements & scale

| Dimension | Target | Consequence |
|---|---|---|
| Edit fidelity | Change only the masked region | Mask-conditioned inpainting; latent blending |
| Personalization | Few images, minutes to train | LoRA/textual-inversion, not full fine-tune |
| Storage | Per-user weights | Tiny LoRA adapters, not full models |
| Serving | Swap adapters at inference | Adapter hot-loading on shared base |

<ThinkFirst question="The base model has never seen the user's dog. What are the candidate ways to teach it — and what does each cost in GPU time and storage?">
Prompting can't do it — no words select a specific unseen subject. The
options: **textual inversion** learns just a new token embedding from 3–5
images (kilobytes, but lower fidelity); **DreamBooth full fine-tuning** binds
the subject into the model weights (best fidelity, ~GPU-hours and a
multi-gigabyte checkpoint *per user*); and the production sweet spot, a
**LoRA adapter** — train low-rank deltas in minutes on one GPU, store a few
megabytes, hot-swap onto a shared base. Rank the fork by fidelity vs.
GPU-minutes vs. bytes-per-user.
</ThinkFirst>

## 3. GenAI problem framing

- **Editing:** conditional diffusion where the condition is the **input image +
  mask** (inpainting), or a natural-language instruction — InstructPix2Pix
  performs instructed edits in a single forward pass, no per-image fine-tuning
  or inversion ([Brooks et al., 2022](https://arxiv.org/abs/2211.09800)).
- **Personalization:** teach the model a new concept from ~3–20 images —
  **DreamBooth** fine-tuning with a unique identifier token
  ([Ruiz et al., 2022](https://arxiv.org/abs/2208.12242)) or **textual
  inversion**, which shows a *single learned embedding vector* can capture a
  concept ([Gal et al., 2022](https://arxiv.org/abs/2208.01618)) — made cheap
  with **LoRA** adapters.

*Pre-generative baseline?* Manual editing (Photoshop) — powerful but slow/skilled;
generative editing makes it a prompt.

<ThinkFirst question="Instructed editing needs (image, instruction, edited-image) triplets at scale. Nobody labels those. Where does the training set come from?">
**Synthesize it with the models you already have.** InstructPix2Pix used
GPT-3 to invent edit instructions and paired captions, then Stable Diffusion
with Prompt-to-Prompt to render ~450K before/after image pairs — a fully
model-generated supervised dataset. The generalization: when a conditioning
task has no natural labels, compose existing generative models into a data
factory, then distill into one feed-forward model. The trained editor
generalizes to real photos it never saw.
</ThinkFirst>

## 4. Data & training *(spine: data)*

- **Editing data:** synthesized (image, mask, edit) or instruction-edit pairs;
  masks are randomly generated during training so the model learns arbitrary
  region completion.
- **Personalization data:** the *user's* few images at request time — a privacy-
  sensitive, per-user training set.
- **Regularization:** DreamBooth's **class-specific prior-preservation loss**
  trains on generated images of the generic class ("a dog") alongside the
  user's subject, preventing language drift — without it, every "dog" the model
  draws becomes *that* dog.

<ThinkFirst question="The user wants an edit that follows a specific pose or edge sketch — 'same scene, but the person stands like this.' Text can't express that. What's the mechanism?">
**Spatial conditioning.** ControlNet bolts a trainable copy of the encoder
onto a frozen diffusion base, connected through zero-initialized convolutions
so training starts as a no-op and can't damage the base; it then injects
edge maps, depth, or human pose as pixel-aligned control signals
([Zhang et al., 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.05543)). The design lesson
generalizes: every new control type is an *add-on branch* on a frozen base —
same economics as LoRA, applied to spatial control instead of identity.
</ThinkFirst>

## 5. Model & generation *(spine: hypothesis-space + optimization)*

- **Base:** a latent diffusion model (shared across users).
- **Personalization via LoRA:** freeze the base, train low-rank update matrices
  — the technique that cut trainable parameters ~10,000× for LLM fine-tuning
  ([Hu et al., 2021](https://arxiv.org/abs/2106.09685)) applies directly to
  diffusion cross-attention: minutes on one GPU, a few MB per subject,
  hot-swappable.
- **Inpainting:** condition the denoiser on the masked latent + surrounding
  context; blend in latent space so edits are seamless.
- **Structural control:** ControlNet-style branches for pose/edges/depth when
  text and masks aren't enough.

<LoRAViz />

LoRA is what makes per-user personalization economical: freeze the giant base,
train a tiny low-rank update, store/serve thousands of them cheaply.

<WikiLink slug="ddim-sampling" title="DDIM sampling for diffusion edits" />

<ThinkFirst question="A million users each have a personalized model. How do you serve that without a million model copies?">
Notice what personalization actually changed: a few megabytes of low-rank
deltas. Keep **one** frozen base model resident on the GPUs and **hot-load the
requesting user's LoRA adapter** per request (fuse or apply as a parallel
branch); personalization training itself is an async job whose output is just
the adapter. "A model per user" becomes "a weight-delta per user" — a million
users at ~5MB each is ~5TB of blob storage, not a million GPU deployments.
That reframing is the entire economics.
</ThinkFirst>

## 6. Serving & scaling

Shared base model resident on GPUs; **hot-load the user's LoRA adapter** for their
request (no per-user model copy). Personalization training runs as an async job
(minutes) → adapter stored keyed to the user. Inpainting requests carry the image
+ mask; batch where possible — though note adapter-per-request works against
cross-user batching, so route same-adapter and adapter-free traffic together.

<ThinkFirst question="Personalization “worked” — by what measure? Two qualities pull against each other here. Which?">
**Identity fidelity vs. editability.** Overtrain the adapter and it memorizes
the training photos — perfect likeness, but every generation is the same pose
and it ignores prompts. Undertrain and it re-poses beautifully but doesn't look
like the subject. Both axes must be evaluated explicitly; optimizing likeness
alone quietly destroys the product's point.
</ThinkFirst>

## 7. Evaluation *(spine: evaluation)*

- **Edit quality:** masked-region realism + **background preservation** (did it
  change only what was asked?), measured by human eval and reference metrics.
- **Personalization:** **identity/subject fidelity** vs. **prompt editability**
  (can you still pose it in new scenes?) — the DreamBooth paper's own protocol:
  DINO/CLIP-image similarity for subject fidelity, CLIP text similarity for
  prompt fidelity, reported *as a pair* because they trade off.
- **Overfitting checks:** generations should not reproduce training photos'
  backgrounds and poses verbatim.

<ThinkFirst question="Generation abuse starts from a prompt. Editing abuse starts from a real photograph. What does that change about the harm — and about your defenses?">
There's now a **victim in the input**. Editing a real photo can strip
clothing, fabricate events, or remove watermarks from someone who actually
exists — harm lands on an identifiable person, and the output inherits a real
image's credibility. Defenses must therefore inspect the *input* image (faces
→ consent gates, provenance/watermark checks before allowing edits), not just
the output — and provenance marking matters doubly, because an edited real
photo is the canonical misinformation artifact.
</ThinkFirst>

## 8. Safety & guardrails

- **Consent & likeness:** personalization on real people is high-risk — require
  consent, block nonconsensual/celebrity personalization (face-match the
  uploaded set), watermark outputs.
- **Edit-specific abuse:** input-image checks for real-person photos,
  watermark-removal and disinformation edits; the same input/output content
  filters as text-to-image still apply.
- **Data privacy:** user images and adapters are private, encrypted at rest,
  and deleted on request — the adapter itself encodes the user's subject.

## 9. Tradeoffs & alternatives

| Choice | Alternative | Why |
|---|---|---|
| LoRA personalization | Full DreamBooth fine-tune | Minutes vs. hours, MBs vs. GBs, hot-swappable |
| Adapter hot-loading | Per-user model copies | One shared base serves everyone |
| Mask-conditioned inpainting | Regenerate whole image | Preserves the untouched regions exactly |
| Textual inversion | LoRA | KBs and even lighter, but usually lower fidelity |

## 10. Interviewer follow-ups

<Details summary="Personalization either doesn't look like the subject or can't be re-posed. Tradeoff?">
Identity fidelity vs. editability. Over-train and the adapter memorizes the exact
training images (great likeness, can't re-pose); under-train and it loses
identity. Tune LoRA rank/steps and add DreamBooth-style prior-preservation
regularization; evaluate both axes explicitly (subject similarity *and* prompt
similarity) rather than optimizing likeness alone.
</Details>

<Details summary="How do you serve millions of personalized users without a model per user?">
One shared frozen base on the GPUs; each user is just a few-MB LoRA adapter
hot-loaded for their request. Personalization is an async training job producing
the adapter. This turns 'a model per user' into 'a tiny weight-delta per user' —
the economic unlock — at the cost of complicating cross-user batching.
</Details>

<Details summary="A user uploads photos of a celebrity to personalize. What happens?">
Block it: consent and likeness policy gates personalization on real
(especially public) people, backed by a face/identity check on the uploaded
training set and output watermarking. Personalization on nonconsensual
likenesses is the signature abuse of this feature, so it's a hard guardrail,
not a soft filter.
</Details>

## Key takeaways

- Image editing/personalization adds **image+mask conditioning** and **per-user
  fine-tuning** on top of a diffusion base; when labels don't exist,
  **synthesize the training data** with the models you have.
- **LoRA adapters + hot-loading** make personalization cheap and scalable — a
  few-MB weight delta per user on one shared base.
- The core quality tradeoff is **identity fidelity vs. editability**, and
  **consent/likeness on real-photo inputs** is the defining safety guardrail.
