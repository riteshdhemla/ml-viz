# Design a Text-to-Image Generation Service

<SystemDesignMeta
  track="Generative AI System Design"
  company="Midjourney / Stability AI"
  scale="millions of images/day · GPU-bound · seconds per image"
  difficulty="intermediate"
/>

A user types "a lighthouse in a storm, oil-painting style, dramatic
light" and, a few seconds and several billion GPU operations later,
four images appear that have never existed before. "Type a prompt, get
an image" is the flagship generative-AI system-design problem — and it
differs from every discriminative system in this course in one
fundamental way: the output is not a label or a score but a **sample
from a learned distribution**. There's no single right answer to check
against, which reshapes all three of the hard problems.

Those three problems organize this case. **Quality** is now
two-dimensional — an image can be gorgeous and ignore the prompt, or
faithful and ugly — and neither axis has an automatic ground truth.
**Cost** is dominated by the physics of generation: a diffusion model
produces each image by running a heavy neural network dozens of times
in a loop, on a GPU, making this the rare product where unit economics
are set by the sampler's step count. And **safety** is intrinsic: a
model that can render anything describable can render things that must
never leave the building. The training pipeline is a standard instance
of the ML loop; what's distinctive is a serving stage that dominates
cost and a data stage that turns out to *be* the product.

<Callout type="info" title="What this case assumes you already know">
This case leans on the generative-models course. The key concepts:

- **Diffusion models** — generate by learning to *reverse* a gradual
  noising process: training teaches the network to denoise; generation
  runs that denoiser from pure noise, step by step (typically 20–50
  steps), each step a full forward pass. Covered in
  generative-models/05.
- **Latent diffusion** — running that denoising loop not on pixels but
  in the compressed latent space of an autoencoder, shrinking every
  step's tensor sizes dramatically. The single biggest cost win in the
  design.
- **Classifier-free guidance (CFG)** — a sampling-time knob that
  pushes generations toward the text condition; higher CFG = more
  prompt adherence, less diversity.
- **Text encoders (CLIP/T5)** — models that turn the prompt into the
  embedding that conditions the denoiser; prompt understanding lives
  here.
- **Samplers & step distillation** — DDIM/DPM-Solver run the reverse
  chain in fewer steps ([DDIM](/wiki/ddim-sampling)); distilled
  "consistency/turbo" models compress it to 1–4 steps.
- **FID** — a metric of *distributional realism*: do generated images
  statistically resemble real ones? **CLIPScore** — a metric of
  *prompt adherence*: does the image match the text? Both defined here
  because their blind spots drive the evaluation section.
- **LoRA fine-tuning** — cheap adaptation of a big base model for
  style or domain, from the fine-tuning course.
- **C2PA / watermarking** — provenance standards marking images as
  AI-generated, visibly or invisibly.
</Callout>

## 1. Clarify the problem

The goal: given a text prompt, generate high-quality, prompt-faithful
images, in seconds, affordably, at product scale — millions of images
a day.

Unpack "high-quality," because it hides the case's central tension:
**aesthetics and prompt adherence are different axes, and they trade
off.** A model tuned hard for beauty drifts toward its favorite
compositions and ignores prompt details; a model that follows every
word can produce faithful, lifeless output. (CFG makes the tradeoff
literal — one scalar slides you along it.) Any evaluation that
collapses the two into one number will mislead.

The product surface adds requirements — resolution tiers, style
controls, variants, editing — and one non-negotiable: **safety**. A
text-to-image system is an anything-renderer; blocking CSAM,
nonconsensual imagery of real people, and IP-infringing output is a
launch requirement, not a fast-follow. Scope: the generation service —
model, serving, safety. Conceptually training is in scope, but the
realistic strategy is fine-tuning an open base model rather than
pretraining from scratch.

<Callout type="tip" title="Interview signal">
Name the two quality axes early — **aesthetics vs. prompt
faithfulness** — and that the system is **GPU-bound at inference**.
Those two facts generate the model story, the serving story, and the
cost story.
</Callout>

## 2. Requirements & scale

| Dimension | Target | Consequence |
|---|---|---|
| Latency | Seconds per image | Fewer denoising steps; distilled/accelerated samplers |
| Cost | GPU is the dominant cost | Batching, step reduction, quantization, latent-space diffusion |
| Quality | Aesthetic + prompt-faithful | Strong text encoder + guidance + fine-tuning |
| Safety | Block harmful/IP-infringing output | Input + output filters, dataset curation |

Do the cost arithmetic, because it structures everything: one image =
*N* denoising steps × one full network forward pass per step × GPU
time per pass. Every cost lever in this design attacks one of those
three factors — fewer steps, cheaper passes, or better GPU
utilization — and the two headline techniques (latent diffusion,
step-distilled samplers) are exactly the biggest attacks on factors
two and one respectively.

## 3. GenAI problem framing

Formally: **conditional generative modeling** — learn the distribution
of images *given* a text description, then sample from it. The
dominant architecture is the **latent diffusion model**: an
autoencoder compresses images into a latent space, a diffusion model
learns to denoise latents conditioned on the prompt embedding, and
**classifier-free guidance** provides the adherence knob at sampling
time.

What was the pre-generative baseline? Two answers, each instructive.
*Retrieval* — search a stock library — which can only ever return
images that already exist; the entire point of generation is the
combinatorial prompt space no library covers. And *GANs* — the
previous generative champion — which are actually *faster* at
inference (one forward pass!) but lost on quality, diversity
(mode collapse), and controllability. Diffusion won by being better,
not cheaper — which is precisely why this case's serving section
exists.

<ThinkFirst question="For a generative model, what does the training data determine beyond output quality?">
Everything the model *can* and *can't* be made to do — its capability
envelope. That makes curation three things at once: the **quality
lever** (dedup, aesthetic filtering, caption quality set the ceiling
on outputs), the **first safety layer** (a model can't readily
generate what it never learned — scrubbing CSAM and exploitative
content from training data is safety at the root), and the **legal
exposure** (training-data provenance and IP are live legal issues;
sources must be documented and opt-outs honored). For generative
systems, dataset curation isn't preprocessing — it's product design.
</ThinkFirst>

## 4. Data & training *(spine: data)*

The ThinkFirst says it: **curation is the product.** The raw material
is web-scale image–text pairs (LAION-style); the work is what happens
to them. Deduplication doesn't just save compute — it directly reduces
*memorization*, the failure where the model regurgitates a training
image nearly verbatim (the IP nightmare scenario). Aesthetic filtering
sets the beauty ceiling. NSFW/CSAM removal is the root-level safety
layer. And caption quality gates prompt adherence — a model trained on
alt-text sludge can't learn fine-grained text-image correspondence no
matter how big it is.

The training strategy for almost everyone except the frontier labs:
**start from an open base model and fine-tune** — full fine-tuning for
a product's overall aesthetic, LoRA for cheap style/brand adapters,
and safety fine-tuning to suppress capabilities the filters shouldn't
have to catch. Pretraining from scratch is a nine-figure decision that
needs its own justification.

## 5. Model & generation *(spine: hypothesis-space + objective)*

Three components, each owning one quality axis:

- **The text encoder** (CLIP/T5-class) turns the prompt into the
  conditioning embedding — and prompt faithfulness lives or dies here:
  if the encoder can't represent "the third house on the left is red,"
  no denoiser downstream can render it.
- **The denoiser** — a UNet or diffusion transformer over latents,
  trained with the noise-prediction objective — owns aesthetic
  quality and visual coherence.
- **The sampler** — DDIM/DPM-Solver at reduced step counts, with CFG
  applied for adherence — owns the speed/quality operating point, and
  step-distilled variants (consistency/turbo models) push toward
  near-real-time generation at a modest quality cost.

The visualization shows the core mechanism: training learns to

<ThinkFirst question="One image costs ~30–50 denoising steps, each a full forward pass on a GPU. Enumerate the cost levers before reading on.">
Attack each factor of the cost product separately. **Fewer steps**:
distilled/consistency samplers collapse 50 steps to 1–4. **Cheaper
steps**: denoise in latent space (smaller tensors per pass), quantize
the network. **Better GPU packing**: batch concurrent requests to
saturate the hardware, autoscale pools on queue depth. And the bonus
lever product teams forget: **caching** — identical trending prompts
recur constantly, and a cached result costs zero GPU-seconds.
</ThinkFirst>

## 6. Serving & scaling

This is the crux for the business. The request path: encode the
prompt → run *N* denoising steps on GPU → decode the latent to
pixels → safety-check → return. The optimizations are the ThinkFirst's
list, deployed together: distilled samplers for step count,
latent-space diffusion and quantization for per-step cost, request
batching for utilization, GPU pools autoscaled by queue depth with
priority tiers (interactive requests jump the queue; bulk/API jobs
absorb the slack), and prompt/seed caching skimming the duplicate
traffic.

The operational point worth making in an interview: unlike an LLM,
image generation is a *job*, not a stream — users tolerate a progress
bar, which is why a queue with tiers works here and why throughput
(images/GPU-hour) rather than tail latency is the primary economic
metric.

<ThinkFirst question="FID improved on the new checkpoint. Ship it? What do FID and CLIPScore each actually measure — and what do both miss?">
Not on FID alone. FID measures **distributional realism** — generated
images statistically resemble real ones. CLIPScore measures **prompt
adherence** — image matches text. Both can improve while humans
prefer the old model, because neither measures the thing users
actually buy: **aesthetic taste** — composition, lighting, style,
appeal. So the pipeline is: automated metrics screen checkpoints
cheaply, and **human preference A/B** makes the ship decision. In
generative systems, automated metrics are gatekeepers, never judges.
</ThinkFirst>

## 7. Evaluation *(spine: evaluation)*

The evaluation stack follows the ThinkFirst's division of labor.
**Automated metrics** — FID for realism, CLIPScore for adherence —
run on every checkpoint and catch regressions early, with the standing
caveat that both correlate only weakly with preference. **Human
preference** — side-by-side A/B rating aesthetics and adherence
separately (the two axes again) — is the real judge, and preference
data is collected continuously because it doubles as training signal
for the feedback stage. **Safety evals** run red-team prompt suites —
jailbreak attempts, celebrity likeness, IP-adjacent prompts — against
every release candidate, because a quality improvement that weakens
refusals is a regression.

## 8. Safety & guardrails

Safety is layered, on the assumption that each layer leaks:

- **Input filtering** blocks prompt classes seeking harmful or illegal
  content before any GPU is spent.
- **Output classification** — NSFW, violence, real-person likeness —
  screens every generated image before return, catching what creative
  prompt-crafting slipped past the input filter.
- **Provenance** — visible marks and invisible watermarks plus C2PA
  metadata — makes outputs identifiable as AI-generated downstream.
- **Memorization monitoring** — near-duplicate search of outputs
  against training data — watches for regurgitation, with
  training-set dedup as the root-cause mitigation.

And beneath all four, the curation layer from section 4: capabilities
the model never learned are the only ones no filter has to catch.

## 9. Feedback & iteration *(spine: feedback)*

The product generates its own training signal: every user choice —
which of the four variants got picked, upscaled, downloaded —
is a preference label. Aggregated, this feeds RLHF-style preference
fine-tuning for aesthetics, closing the loop between what users
actually like and what the model produces next. Operationally,
prompt trends shift constantly (new styles, new memes), so
safety-filter hit rates and rejection rates are monitored as drift
signals — a rising rejection rate means either a new abuse pattern or
a filter that's aged out of calibration, and the two need opposite
responses.

## 10. Tradeoffs & alternatives

| Choice | Alternative | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Latent diffusion | Pixel-space diffusion | Denoising compressed latents is far cheaper per step |
| Diffusion | GANs | Better quality/diversity/control; GANs are faster but unstable |
| Step-distilled sampling | Full 50-step sampling | Cuts GPU cost/latency dramatically at slight quality loss |
| Fine-tune open base | Train from scratch | Orders of magnitude cheaper; open bases are strong |

## 11. Interviewer follow-ups

<Details summary="Images look great but ignore half the prompt. How do you improve adherence?">
Work the adherence axis directly: raise the CFG scale (buying
adherence with diversity — the explicit tradeoff), strengthen the
text encoder (adherence is bounded by what the conditioning can
represent), and fine-tune on high-quality caption–image pairs so the
model learns tighter text-image correspondence. Measure with
CLIPScore plus human adherence ratings scored separately from
aesthetics — FID can't see this problem at all, since a beautiful
wrong image is still distributionally realistic.
</Details>

<Details summary="Generation takes 8 seconds and GPU costs are killing us. What do you do?">
Attack the cost identity — steps × cost-per-step × utilization — on
all three factors: swap in a distilled/consistency sampler (50 steps →
1–4, the biggest single win), ensure denoising runs in latent space
and quantize the network (cheaper passes), and batch requests to
saturate GPUs (utilization). Then add caching for duplicate prompts.
Expect to trade a small, human-evaluated quality delta for the step
reduction — which is why the preference A/B from evaluation gates the
rollout.
</Details>

<Details summary="A user prompts for a real celebrity in a compromising scene. How is that blocked?">
By depth, not by one gate: the input filter rejects the prompt class;
the output classifier checks generated images for disallowed likeness
and NSFW before return; and beneath both, data curation and safety
fine-tuning have reduced the model's ability to produce it in the
first place. Each layer catches what the previous one leaks — creative
rephrasings beat input filters, so the output check exists; output
checks err, so capability suppression matters. Watermarking adds
after-the-fact accountability for anything that escapes all three.
</Details>

## Key takeaways

- Text-to-image is **conditional generative modeling** with two
  quality axes — **aesthetics vs. prompt faithfulness** — that trade
  off and must be measured separately; human preference, not FID,
  makes ship decisions.
- The economics are the sampler's: **cost = steps × per-step cost ×
  utilization**, so latent diffusion, step distillation, quantization,
  and batching are the levers that make the product viable.
- **Dataset curation is product design**: it sets the quality ceiling,
  forms the root safety layer, and carries the legal exposure.
- Safety is **defense in depth** — curation, input filters, output
  classifiers, watermarking — because every individual layer leaks.
