# Design a Text-to-Image Generation Service

<SystemDesignMeta
  track="Generative AI System Design"
  company="Midjourney / Stability AI"
  scale="millions of images/day · GPU-bound · seconds per image"
  difficulty="intermediate"
/>

"Type a prompt, get an image" is the flagship generative-AI system-design
problem. Unlike a classifier, the output is a **sample from a learned
distribution**, so the challenges are generation *quality*, **GPU cost** (the
model runs a heavy denoising loop), and **safety** (it can generate anything).
Most of the pipeline is an instance of the ML loop (train a generative model),
with a serving stage that dominates cost.

## 1. Clarify the problem

- **Goal:** given a text prompt, generate high-quality, prompt-faithful images,
  fast and affordably, at product scale.
- **Quality bar:** aesthetic quality *and* prompt adherence (the image matches
  what was asked) — two axes that trade off.
- **Product surface:** resolution, styles, editing, number of variants; safety is
  non-negotiable (no CSAM, no nonconsensual imagery, IP concerns).
- **Scope:** the generation service (model + serving + safety). Training the base
  model is in scope conceptually; in practice you'd fine-tune an open base like
  SDXL rather than train from scratch.

<Callout type="tip" title="Interview signal">
Name the two quality axes early — **aesthetics vs. prompt faithfulness** — and
that the system is **GPU-bound** at inference. Those two facts drive the model,
serving, and cost story.
</Callout>

<ThinkFirst question="Stable Diffusion 1.x runs an ~860M-parameter UNet ~50 times per image. Walk the arithmetic: roughly how long is one image on a modern datacenter GPU, how many images per GPU-hour — and what fleet does 'millions of images/day' imply?">
Each denoising step is a full forward pass, so time ≈ steps × per-step cost —
a few seconds per image at 50 steps, i.e. on the order of 1–2K images per
GPU-hour. Millions of images a day therefore means a fleet of hundreds of
GPUs running hot. And because cost is *linear in step count*, halving steps
halves the fleet, which is why step reduction, not model shrinking, is the
first cost lever every image product pulls.
</ThinkFirst>

## 2. Requirements & scale

| Dimension | Target | Consequence |
|---|---|---|
| Latency | Seconds per image | Fewer denoising steps; distilled/accelerated samplers |
| Cost | GPU is the dominant cost | Batching, step reduction, quantization, latent-space diffusion |
| Quality | Aesthetic + prompt-faithful | Strong text encoder + guidance + fine-tuning |
| Safety | Block harmful/IP-infringing output | Input + output filters, dataset curation |

**Back-of-envelope:** a diffusion model runs *N* denoising steps, each a full
UNet/transformer forward pass on a GPU. Total cost is
$N \times \text{cost}(\text{step})$, so the whole cost story is "how do we cut
$N$, shrink each step, and pack GPUs", which is why **latent** diffusion
(denoise a compressed representation) and step-distilled samplers matter so
much.

## 3. GenAI problem framing

**Conditional generative modeling.** The dominant family is the **latent
diffusion model**: an autoencoder compresses images into a latent space and a
diffusion model learns to denoise latents conditioned on a text embedding
([Rombach et al., 2022](https://arxiv.org/abs/2112.10752)). At sampling time,
**classifier-free guidance (CFG)** extrapolates away from the unconditional
prediction,

$$\hat{\epsilon} = \epsilon_{\text{uncond}} + w\,(\epsilon_{\text{cond}} - \epsilon_{\text{uncond}}),$$

trading diversity for prompt adherence as the guidance scale $w$ (typically
5–8) rises ([Ho & Salimans, 2022](https://arxiv.org/abs/2207.12598)).

*What was the pre-generative baseline?* Retrieval (search a stock library) or GANs
— GANs are fast but unstable and mode-collapse-prone; diffusion won on quality and
controllability.

<ThinkFirst question="For a generative model, what does the training data determine beyond output quality?">
Everything the model *can* and *can't* be made to do. Curation — dedup,
aesthetic filtering, NSFW/CSAM removal, caption quality — is simultaneously the
quality lever, the first safety layer (a model can't easily generate what it
never learned), and the legal exposure (training-data provenance and IP). For
generative systems, dataset curation isn't preprocessing; it's product design.
</ThinkFirst>

## 4. Data & training *(spine: data)*

- **Data:** web-scale image–text pairs — LAION-5B is the canonical open corpus:
  5.85B CLIP-filtered pairs, shipped with NSFW, watermark, and toxicity
  detection scores precisely so trainers can filter
  ([Schuhmann et al., 2022](https://arxiv.org/abs/2210.08402)). **Curation is
  the product**: dedup, aesthetic filtering, CSAM removal, and caption quality
  gate what the model can do, and what it *can't* be made to do.
- **IP/licensing:** training-data provenance is a legal and ethical minefield;
  document sources and honor opt-outs (Stability and others now run artist
  opt-out programs).
- **Fine-tuning:** start from an open base, then fine-tune / LoRA for style,
  brand, or safety; aesthetic fine-tuning on a small curated set moves human
  preference more than architecture changes.

<WikiLink slug="ddim-sampling" title="DDIM — fast deterministic diffusion sampling" />

<ThinkFirst question="Stable Diffusion's autoencoder maps a 512×512×3 image to a 64×64×4 latent. Compute the reduction factor — and name what you risk losing by denoising there instead of in pixel space.">
786K values down to 16K — a ~48× reduction that every one of the ~50
denoising passes enjoys. That, more than any sampler trick, is what made
text-to-image affordable. The price: final quality is capped by the VAE
decoder, so high-frequency detail the 8×-downsampled latent can't carry —
legible text, faces, hands — becomes the signature failure mode, and decoder
upgrades ship as quality wins on their own.
</ThinkFirst>

## 5. Model & generation *(spine: hypothesis-space + objective)*

- **Text encoder:** prompt faithfulness lives or dies here. Imagen showed that
  scaling a frozen T5-XXL text encoder improved both fidelity and image–text
  alignment *more* than scaling the diffusion model itself
  ([Saharia et al., 2022](https://arxiv.org/abs/2205.11487)); SDXL runs two
  text encoders (CLIP ViT-L + OpenCLIP ViT-bigG) feeding a 2.6B-parameter UNet
  ([Podell et al., 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2307.01952)).
- **Denoiser:** a UNet or diffusion transformer over latents, trained with the
  denoising (noise-prediction) objective.
- **Sampling:** DDIM/DPM-Solver at 20–50 steps + CFG for adherence; **step
  distillation** collapses this — adversarial diffusion distillation (SDXL
  Turbo) samples in 1–4 steps at near-teacher quality
  ([Sauer et al., 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.17042)).

<DiffusionViz />

Diffusion learns to reverse a gradual noising process — generation is running that
reverse chain from pure noise, guided by the text embedding.

<ThinkFirst question="One image costs 20–50 denoising steps, each a full forward pass on a GPU. Enumerate the cost levers before reading on — and estimate what each is worth.">
Attack each factor of the product: **fewer steps** (distillation gets 50 →
1–4, a ~10–25× win), **cheaper steps** (the ~48× latent compression, plus
quantizing the denoiser), and **better GPU packing** (batch concurrent
requests, autoscale by queue depth). Caching — identical prompts, reusable
text-encoder embeddings, seeds for variants — is the bonus lever product
teams forget. Note the levers multiply, and step count dominates.
</ThinkFirst>

## 6. Serving & scaling

The crux. Per request: encode prompt → run *N* denoising steps on GPU → decode
latent → safety-check → return. Optimize by:

- **Fewer steps** (distilled samplers: 50 → 1–4), **latent-space** diffusion
  (~48× smaller tensors per step), **quantization** (fp16/int8 denoiser), and
  **batching** concurrent requests to saturate GPUs.
- **Autoscaling** GPU pools by queue depth; a job queue with priority tiers
  (interactive draft vs. batch upscale) — Midjourney's "fast/relax" modes are
  exactly this split.
- **Caching** text-encoder embeddings and identical prompt+seed results.

<ThinkFirst question="FID improved on the new checkpoint. Ship it? What do FID and CLIPScore each actually measure — and what do both miss?">
FID measures **distributional realism** (do generated images statistically
resemble real ones); CLIPScore measures **prompt adherence**. Neither measures
what users buy: aesthetic taste. Both can improve while humans prefer the old
model, so automated metrics screen checkpoints, and **human preference A/B**
makes the ship call.
</ThinkFirst>

## 7. Evaluation *(spine: evaluation)*

- **Automated:** **FID** (distributional realism) and **CLIPScore** (prompt
  adherence) — necessary but weakly correlated with human taste; run them on a
  fixed prompt suite spanning composition, counting, text rendering, and style.
- **Human preference:** side-by-side A/B on aesthetics + adherence is the real
  judge; collect preference data continuously from user picks and upscales.
- **Safety evals:** red-team prompt suites for harmful/IP-infringing
  generations, tracked per policy category.

<ThinkFirst question="A generation comes back with a stock-photo watermark ghosted into the corner. The model has no retrieval component. What happened — and which training-time decision most reduces it?">
The model **memorized**. Diffusion models trained on billions of web images
can emit near-copies of training examples — Carlini et al. extracted over a
thousand training images from Stable Diffusion with a generate-and-filter
attack, and found heavily *duplicated* training images are by far the
likeliest to regurgitate. So deduplication is the highest-leverage
mitigation, and extraction-style audits belong in your eval suite. This is
an IP and privacy exposure, not a curiosity.
</ThinkFirst>

## 8. Safety & guardrails

- **Input filtering:** classify prompts and block clearly disallowed requests
  before spending a generation.
- **Output filtering:** a classifier on generated images (CSAM, NSFW, violence,
  likeness of real people) before returning. DALL·E 3 ships exactly this
  layered stack — external red-teaming plus prompt- and image-level classifiers
  with category-specific handling
  ([OpenAI, 2023](https://openai.com/index/dall-e-3-system-card/)).
- **Provenance:** watermark outputs (visible/invisible + C2PA signed metadata)
  so AI images are identifiable downstream.
- **Memorization/IP:** dedup training data and monitor for training-image
  regurgitation ([Carlini et al., 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.13188)).

## 9. Feedback & iteration *(spine: feedback)*

- **Preference data** from user picks/upscales feeds fine-tuning (RLHF-style
  alignment for aesthetics/safety) — the pick-one-of-four UI is a preference
  labeler in disguise.
- **Drift:** prompt trends shift; monitor rejection/safety-filter rates and
  refresh models.

## 10. Tradeoffs & alternatives

| Choice | Alternative | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Latent diffusion | Pixel-space diffusion | ~48× smaller tensors per denoising step |
| Diffusion | GANs | Better quality/diversity/control; GANs are faster but unstable |
| Distilled 1–4-step sampling | Full 50-step sampling | ~10–25× less GPU per image at slight quality loss |
| Fine-tune open base | Train from scratch | Orders of magnitude cheaper; base models are strong |

## 11. Interviewer follow-ups

<Details summary="Images look great but ignore half the prompt. How do you improve adherence?">
Prompt faithfulness is driven by the text encoder, the captions, and guidance.
Raise the CFG scale (more adherence, less diversity, but too high causes
oversaturated artifacts), use a stronger/larger text encoder (the Imagen
result), and fine-tune on high-quality caption–image pairs. Measure with
CLIPScore + human adherence ratings, not just FID (which only captures
realism).
</Details>

<Details summary="Generation takes 8 seconds and GPU costs are killing us. What do you do?">
Cut denoising steps with a distilled sampler (adversarial or consistency
distillation reaches 1–4 steps), stay in latent space, quantize the denoiser,
and batch requests to saturate GPUs. Each step is a full forward pass, so
fewer steps × smaller tensors × better packing is the whole cost lever — and
step count is the dominant factor.
</Details>

<Details summary="A user prompts for a real celebrity in a compromising scene. How is that blocked?">
Layered: an input filter rejects the prompt class, an output classifier flags
disallowed likeness/NSFW before returning, and training data + fine-tuning reduce
the model's ability to produce it. No single layer is sufficient — safety is
defense-in-depth, and provenance watermarking aids downstream accountability.
</Details>

## Key takeaways

- Text-to-image is **conditional generative modeling** (latent diffusion + CFG) —
  the axes are **aesthetics vs. prompt faithfulness**.
- The system is **GPU-bound**: fewer steps (distillation to 1–4), latent-space
  diffusion (~48× compression), quantization, and batching are the cost levers —
  and they multiply.
- **Data curation** defines capability, safety, and memorization risk; **layered
  input/output filtering + watermarking** are mandatory, not optional.
