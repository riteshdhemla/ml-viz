# Design a Text-to-Video Generation System

<SystemDesignMeta
  track="Generative AI System Design"
  company="OpenAI Sora / Runway"
  scale="minutes of GPU per clip · temporal consistency · massive storage"
  difficulty="advanced"
/>

Text-to-video is text-to-image with a brutal extra dimension: **time**. A video is
dozens of frames that must be individually realistic *and* temporally coherent
(objects persist, motion is smooth), which explodes compute, memory, and storage.
It's the frontier generative-AI system-design problem — the answer is all about
**taming the temporal + compute cost**.

## 1. Clarify the problem

- **Goal:** generate a short, coherent video clip from a text prompt (optionally an
  image/first-frame), temporally consistent and prompt-faithful.
- **The new hard axis:** **temporal consistency** — no flicker, stable identities,
  physically plausible motion — on top of per-frame quality.
- **Compute reality:** generation is minutes of GPU per clip; training is at the
  extreme end of distributed compute.
- **Scope:** the generation pipeline (model + serving + safety). Assumes the
  text-to-image case as the spatial foundation.

<Callout type="tip" title="Interview signal">
"Video = image generation + temporal consistency, and the cost is dominated by the
time dimension." Framing temporal coherence and compute/storage as *the* problems
(not per-frame quality) is the differentiator.
</Callout>

## 2. Requirements & scale

| Dimension | Target | Consequence |
|---|---|---|
| Temporal consistency | Smooth, flicker-free, stable identities | Spatiotemporal attention; latent video model |
| Compute | Minutes of GPU per clip | Latent + spacetime compression; staged generation |
| Storage/bandwidth | Videos are huge | Aggressive latent compression; CDN |
| Latency | Async (not interactive) | Job queue; progress streaming |

<ThinkFirst question="You already have a great text-to-image model. Why not just run it once per frame?">
Because each frame would be an *independent* sample from the distribution — the
dog changes breed between frames, textures flicker, the camera teleports.
Temporal coherence requires frames to be generated **jointly**, which is why
video models denoise a whole spacetime latent with temporal attention rather
than looping an image model.
</ThinkFirst>

## 3. GenAI problem framing

**Spatiotemporal latent diffusion.** Encode the video into a compressed
**spacetime latent** (compress both spatially and temporally), then run a
diffusion transformer with **spatial + temporal attention** so frames are
generated jointly, not independently. Generation is diffusion over the whole clip's
latent.

*Pre-generative baseline?* Frame-by-frame image generation — fails badly:
independent frames flicker and drift. Joint spacetime modeling is what buys
coherence.

## 4. Data & training *(spine: optimization)*

- **Data:** large video–text corpora with quality/caption filtering; captioning
  videos (often with a VLM) is itself a subsystem.
- **Compute:** training needs **distributed training at scale** — data/tensor/
  pipeline parallelism, gradient checkpointing, huge clusters. This is where the
  money goes.
- **Curation:** dedup, resolution/FPS normalization, safety filtering of training
  video.

<WikiLink slug="ddim-sampling" title="Diffusion sampling (extended to video latents)" />

<ThinkFirst question="Full attention over every spacetime patch of a 10-second clip — feasible? Where does the cost go, and what do you factor?">
Attention is quadratic in tokens, and video multiplies tokens by the frame
count — full 3D attention is prohibitive. Two factorizations tame it:
**separate spatial and temporal attention** (attend within a frame, then
across time at each location), and **staged generation** — produce a
low-res/low-FPS clip first, then super-resolve spatially and interpolate
temporally. Never pay full price in one pass.
</ThinkFirst>

## 5. Model & generation *(spine: hypothesis-space)*

- **Architecture:** a diffusion transformer over spacetime latent patches with
  factorized spatial/temporal attention (full 3D attention is too expensive).
- **Conditioning:** text (and optionally a first frame/image) via cross-attention;
  CFG for adherence.
- **Staged generation:** generate a low-res/low-FPS clip, then **super-resolve**
  spatially and **interpolate** temporally — cheaper than generating full-res
  directly.

<DiffusionViz />

The same denoising idea as images, but the model denoises a *spacetime* latent so
motion and identity stay coherent across frames.

## 6. Serving & scaling

Inherently **async**: a request is a GPU job (minutes) on a queue with progress
streaming, not an interactive call. Multi-GPU per clip for large models;
autoscale GPU pools by queue depth. Outputs are large — store in object storage,
serve via CDN, transcode to delivery formats.

<ThinkFirst question="Every individual frame scores beautifully on FID. Is the video good?">
Not necessarily — per-frame metrics are blind to time. A clip can have
photorealistic frames that flicker, drift, and morph between them. Video needs
**temporal** metrics: FVD (distributional realism over clips, not frames),
warp/flicker consistency, motion smoothness — and ultimately human judgment,
because "does it look real over time" is what viewers actually perceive.
</ThinkFirst>

## 7. Evaluation *(spine: evaluation)*

- **Per-frame quality:** FID/aesthetic scores.
- **Temporal metrics:** FVD (Fréchet Video Distance), warp/flicker consistency,
  motion smoothness.
- **Prompt adherence:** CLIP-style video–text alignment.
- **Human eval** dominates — coherence and "does it look real over time" are what
  people judge; collect preference data.

## 8. Safety & guardrails

- **Deepfakes:** likeness of real people is the top risk — block/consent-gate,
  watermark (visible + C2PA provenance).
- **Content safety:** input/output filtering as with images, extended to
  temporal content.
- **IP:** training-data provenance and memorization monitoring.

## 9. Tradeoffs & alternatives

| Choice | Alternative | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Joint spacetime diffusion | Frame-by-frame image gen | Independent frames flicker/drift; joint gives coherence |
| Spacetime latent compression | Pixel-space video | Pixels are astronomically expensive at video scale |
| Staged (low-res → SR → interpolate) | Direct full-res generation | Far cheaper for the same output quality |
| Factorized space/time attention | Full 3D attention | 3D attention cost is prohibitive |

## 10. Interviewer follow-ups

<Details summary="Frames look great individually but the video flickers. Why, and fix?">
You're effectively generating frames too independently. Add/strengthen temporal
attention so frames are denoised jointly, condition later frames on earlier latent
state, and train with temporal-consistency objectives. Measure FVD/flicker, not
just per-frame FID — per-frame quality can be high while temporal coherence is
poor.
</Details>

<Details summary="Generating full-res video directly is impossibly expensive. Alternatives?">
Stage it: generate a low-resolution, low-FPS clip in a compressed spacetime
latent, then spatially super-resolve and temporally interpolate. Each stage is
much cheaper than direct high-res generation, and the latent compression keeps
tensors tractable. This cascade is standard for video and high-res image models.
</Details>

<Details summary="How do you prevent realistic deepfakes of real people?">
Consent/likeness gating on prompts and reference images, output classifiers for
recognizable real individuals, and mandatory provenance watermarking (visible +
C2PA metadata). Video deepfakes are the highest-stakes abuse here, so it's
layered prevention plus traceability, not a single filter.
</Details>

## Key takeaways

- Text-to-video is **spatiotemporal diffusion** — the defining challenge is
  **temporal consistency**, not per-frame quality.
- **Spacetime latent compression + staged generation** (low-res → super-res →
  interpolate) tame otherwise impossible compute and storage costs.
- Evaluate with **temporal metrics (FVD/flicker)** and human preference; **deepfake
  prevention + provenance watermarking** are the critical guardrails.
