# Design a Text-to-Video Generation System

<SystemDesignMeta
  track="Generative AI System Design"
  company="OpenAI Sora / Runway"
  scale="minutes of GPU per clip · temporal consistency · massive storage"
  difficulty="advanced"
/>

Text-to-video is text-to-image with a brutal extra dimension: **time**. A video is
hundreds of frames that must be individually realistic *and* temporally coherent
(objects persist, motion is smooth), which explodes compute, memory, and storage.
It's the frontier generative-AI system-design problem — production systems like
Sora, Veo, and Runway all converge on the same answer: **tame the temporal +
compute cost with compression, factorization, and staging**.

## 1. Clarify the problem

- **Goal:** generate a short, coherent video clip from a text prompt (optionally an
  image/first-frame), temporally consistent and prompt-faithful.
- **The new hard axis:** **temporal consistency** — no flicker, stable identities,
  physically plausible motion — on top of per-frame quality.
- **Compute reality:** generation is minutes of GPU per clip; training is at the
  extreme end of distributed compute.
- **Scope:** the generation pipeline (model + serving + safety). Assumes the
  text-to-image case as the spatial foundation.

<Callout type="tip" title="Interview signal">
"Video = image generation + temporal consistency, and the cost is dominated by the
time dimension." Framing temporal coherence and compute/storage as *the* problems
(not per-frame quality) is the differentiator.
</Callout>

## 2. Requirements & scale

| Dimension | Target | Consequence |
|---|---|---|
| Temporal consistency | Smooth, flicker-free, stable identities | Spatiotemporal attention; latent video model |
| Compute | Minutes of GPU per clip | Latent + spacetime compression; staged generation |
| Storage/bandwidth | Videos are huge | Aggressive latent compression; CDN |
| Latency | Async (not interactive) | Job queue; progress streaming |

**Back-of-envelope:** a 16-second 1080p clip at 16 fps is 256 frames — even a
single frame equals an entire text-to-image request, and the frames can't be
made independently. Meta's Movie Gen represents such a clip as ~73K latent
tokens inside a 30B-parameter transformer
([Movie Gen team, 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.13720)) — context lengths
and model sizes that dictate everything downstream.

<ThinkFirst question="You already have a great text-to-image model. Why not just run it once per frame?">
Because each frame would be an *independent* sample from the distribution — the
dog changes breed between frames, textures flicker, the camera teleports.
Temporal coherence requires frames to be generated **jointly**, which is why
video models denoise a whole spacetime latent with temporal attention rather
than looping an image model.
</ThinkFirst>

## 3. GenAI problem framing

**Spatiotemporal latent diffusion.** Encode the video into a compressed
**spacetime latent** (compress both spatially and temporally), then run a
diffusion transformer over **spacetime patches** so frames are generated
jointly, not independently. This is Sora's core recipe — a DiT trained on
spacetime patches of video latents, at variable durations, resolutions, and
aspect ratios
([OpenAI, 2024](https://openai.com/index/video-generation-models-as-world-simulators/)).

*Pre-generative baseline?* Frame-by-frame image generation — fails badly:
independent frames flicker and drift. Joint spacetime modeling is what buys
coherence.

<ThinkFirst question="You've scraped a billion web videos. List what must happen before a single training step runs — and identify the preprocessing step that is itself a frontier ML system.">
Cut raw footage into single-shot clips (scene detection), filter for quality
(resolution, motion, text overlays, aesthetics), dedup, safety-screen — and
then **caption everything densely**, because alt-text for video barely
exists. That captioning step is a video-language model in its own right:
Sora applies the DALL·E 3 re-captioning technique to video, and Stable Video
Diffusion showed that this curation stage matters as much as architecture —
its recipe cut a ~600M-clip corpus down before high-quality finetuning.
</ThinkFirst>

## 4. Data & training *(spine: optimization)*

- **Data:** large video–text corpora; captioning with a VLM is a subsystem, and
  curation is staged. Stable Video Diffusion's recipe is explicit: image
  pretraining → large-scale video pretraining on a curated ~600M-clip dataset →
  high-quality video finetuning, with ablations showing the curation stages
  drive the win ([Blattmann et al., 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.15127)).
- **Compute:** training needs **distributed training at scale** — data/tensor/
  pipeline parallelism, gradient checkpointing, huge clusters. This is where the
  money goes.
- **Curation:** dedup, resolution/FPS normalization, cut detection, safety
  filtering of training video.

<WikiLink slug="ddim-sampling" title="Diffusion sampling (extended to video latents)" />

<ThinkFirst question="Full attention over every spacetime patch of a 10-second clip — feasible? Where does the cost go, and what do you factor?">
Attention is quadratic in tokens, and video multiplies tokens by the frame
count — full 3D attention over hundreds of thousands of patches is
prohibitive. Two factorizations tame it: **separate spatial and temporal
attention** (attend within a frame, then across time at each location), and
**staged generation** — produce a low-res/low-FPS clip first, then
super-resolve spatially and interpolate temporally. Never pay full price in
one pass.
</ThinkFirst>

## 5. Model & generation *(spine: hypothesis-space)*

- **Architecture:** a diffusion transformer over spacetime latent patches;
  smaller models factorize spatial/temporal attention, while the largest (Sora,
  Movie Gen's 30B flow-matching transformer) afford joint attention over the
  full latent — the frontier is exactly where that line sits.
- **Conditioning:** text (and optionally a first frame/image) via
  cross-attention; CFG for adherence.
- **Staged generation:** generate a low-res/low-FPS clip, then **super-resolve**
  spatially and **interpolate** temporally — cheaper than generating full-res
  directly (Movie Gen generates at 768px and upsamples to 1080p).

<DiffusionViz />

The same denoising idea as images, but the model denoises a *spacetime* latent so
motion and identity stay coherent across frames.

<ThinkFirst question="A 30B-parameter model with a 73K-token context takes minutes to produce 16 seconds of video. Design the request path — and say why 'interactive latency' is the wrong goal.">
Treat every generation as an **async GPU job**: enqueue, schedule onto a
multi-GPU worker (a 30B model at that context won't fit a single GPU's
memory + KV budget), stream progress and a low-res preview, notify on
completion. Users tolerate minutes for a clip they'll keep; they don't
tolerate a spinner with no feedback. So the system optimizes **throughput
per GPU-hour and queue fairness** (priority tiers, preemption), not p50
latency — the opposite of a chat-serving stack.
</ThinkFirst>

## 6. Serving & scaling

Inherently **async**: a request is a GPU job (minutes) on a queue with progress
streaming, not an interactive call. Multi-GPU inference per clip for the largest
models; autoscale GPU pools by queue depth; priority tiers (draft vs. final
render). Outputs are large — store in object storage, serve via CDN, transcode
to delivery formats. Charge per generated second: unit economics are visible in
this product like almost no other.

<ThinkFirst question="Every individual frame scores beautifully on FID. Is the video good?">
Not necessarily — per-frame metrics are blind to time. A clip can have
photorealistic frames that flicker, drift, and morph between them. Video needs
**temporal** metrics: FVD (distributional realism over clips, not frames),
warp/flicker consistency, motion smoothness, and ultimately human judgment,
because "does it look real over time" is what viewers actually perceive.
</ThinkFirst>

## 7. Evaluation *(spine: evaluation)*

- **Per-frame quality:** FID/aesthetic scores.
- **Temporal metrics:** **FVD** (Fréchet Video Distance) — an FID analogue
  computed on features from a video network (an I3D action classifier), so it
  sees temporal coherence, and it correlates with human judgment
  ([Unterthiner et al., 2018](https://arxiv.org/abs/1812.01717)); plus
  warp/flicker consistency and motion smoothness.
- **Prompt adherence:** video–text alignment plus physics/object-permanence
  probes — the Sora report evaluates exactly these emergent behaviors.
- **Human eval** dominates — Movie Gen shipped a human A/B benchmark
  (MovieGenBench) precisely because automated metrics under-measure coherence.

<ThinkFirst question="Deepfake detectors keep losing to better generators. As the platform generating the video, what stronger position can you take than post-hoc detection?">
**Provenance at generation time.** A detector fights an arms race it loses as
models improve; the generator, by contrast, can mark every output at the
source — an invisible watermark in the pixels plus cryptographically signed
C2PA metadata. Neither survives a determined adversary (metadata dies on
screenshot; watermarks can be attacked), but together they make honest
distribution channels verifiable and raise the cost of laundering, which is
the realistic goal. Detection is a fallback, not the plan.
</ThinkFirst>

## 8. Safety & guardrails

- **Deepfakes:** likeness of real people is the top risk — block/consent-gate
  prompts and reference images; watermark and attach C2PA provenance to every
  output (Sora and Veo both ship watermark + metadata provenance).
- **Content safety:** input/output filtering as with images, extended to
  temporal content (an innocuous frame sequence can compose into a violent
  action).
- **IP:** training-data provenance and memorization monitoring.

## 9. Tradeoffs & alternatives

| Choice | Alternative | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Joint spacetime diffusion | Frame-by-frame image gen | Independent frames flicker/drift; joint gives coherence |
| Spacetime latent compression | Pixel-space video | Pixels are astronomically expensive at video scale |
| Staged (low-res → SR → interpolate) | Direct full-res generation | Far cheaper for the same output quality |
| Factorized space/time attention | Full 3D attention | Quadratic cost; full 3D only affordable at frontier scale |

## 10. Interviewer follow-ups

<Details summary="Frames look great individually but the video flickers. Why, and fix?">
You're effectively generating frames too independently. Strengthen temporal
attention so frames are denoised jointly, condition later frames on earlier latent
state, and train with temporal-consistency objectives. Measure FVD/flicker, not
just per-frame FID — per-frame quality can be high while temporal coherence is
poor.
</Details>

<Details summary="Generating full-res video directly is impossibly expensive. Alternatives?">
Stage it: generate a low-resolution, low-FPS clip in a compressed spacetime
latent, then spatially super-resolve and temporally interpolate — Movie Gen's
768px→1080p upsampling is this pattern in production. Each stage is much
cheaper than direct high-res generation, and the latent compression keeps
tensors tractable. This cascade is standard for video and high-res image
models.
</Details>

<Details summary="How do you prevent realistic deepfakes of real people?">
Consent/likeness gating on prompts and reference images, output classifiers for
recognizable real individuals, and mandatory provenance (invisible watermark +
C2PA signed metadata) on everything generated. Video deepfakes are the
highest-stakes abuse here, so it's layered prevention plus traceability, not a
single filter.
</Details>

## Key takeaways

- Text-to-video is **spatiotemporal diffusion over spacetime latent patches** —
  the defining challenge is **temporal consistency**, not per-frame quality.
- **Spacetime latent compression + staged generation** (low-res → super-res →
  interpolate) tame otherwise impossible compute; serving is **async jobs
  optimized for throughput**, not latency.
- Evaluate with **temporal metrics (FVD/flicker)** and human preference;
  **provenance at generation time** (watermark + C2PA) beats post-hoc deepfake
  detection.
