# Design a Text-to-Video Generation System

<SystemDesignMeta
  track="Generative AI System Design"
  company="OpenAI Sora / Runway"
  scale="minutes of GPU per clip · temporal consistency · massive storage"
  difficulty="advanced"
/>

Suppose you already own a superb text-to-image model. Video is just
many images — so run the model once per frame and stitch the results?
Try it, and the failure is instructive: the golden retriever in frame
one is a different dog in frame two, textures shimmer and crawl, the
camera teleports. Every frame is beautiful; the *video* is unwatchable.

That failed experiment contains the whole case. Video is text-to-image
plus one brutal extra dimension — **time** — and time is not a loop
over frames. The frames of a real video are deeply correlated
(objects persist, motion continues, lighting evolves smoothly), so
they must be generated **jointly**, as one coherent spacetime object.
Doing that multiplies every cost that was already painful in image
generation: attention over frames × patches explodes compute, training
sits at the extreme end of distributed infrastructure, a single clip
takes minutes of GPU, and the outputs are enormous. Text-to-video is
the frontier generative-AI systems problem, and the design is one long
exercise in **taming the temporal and compute cost** without giving up
the coherence that was the point.

<Callout type="info" title="What this case assumes you already know">
This case stacks directly on the text-to-image case; read that first.
The additional concepts:

- **Latent diffusion & CFG** — denoising in a compressed latent
  space, guided toward the text condition; the spatial foundation,
  from the text-to-image case and generative-models/05.
- **Diffusion transformers & patches** — treating a latent as a
  sequence of patch tokens processed by a transformer; video extends
  the patches across time ("spacetime patches").
- **Attention's quadratic cost** — attention scales with the square
  of token count; the reason full 3D (space × time) attention is
  prohibitive and factorization is required.
- **Temporal consistency** — the video-specific quality axis: no
  flicker, stable object identities, physically plausible motion.
- **Super-resolution & frame interpolation** — models that upscale
  spatially and synthesize in-between frames; the second and third
  stages of cascaded generation.
- **FVD (Fréchet Video Distance)** — FID's video counterpart,
  computed over *clips* so it can see temporal failures that
  per-frame metrics miss.
- **Distributed training** — data/tensor/pipeline parallelism and
  gradient checkpointing, from the GPU course; video training is
  among its most extreme customers.
- **C2PA / watermarking** — provenance marking, here at its highest
  stakes: video deepfakes.
</Callout>

## 1. Clarify the problem

The goal: generate a short, coherent video clip from a text prompt —
optionally conditioned on an image or first frame — that is
temporally consistent and prompt-faithful.

The new hard axis is **temporal consistency**, and it's worth
spelling out what it contains: no flicker (textures stable across
frames), persistent identities (the same dog throughout), and
physically plausible motion (things move, they don't teleport). This
axis is *additional to* per-frame quality, and — the interview-signal
point — it's the harder and more distinctive of the two.

The compute reality frames everything else: generation costs minutes
of GPU per clip (so serving is asynchronous by nature), and training
sits at the far end of distributed-systems scale (so the data and
optimization stages are dominated by infrastructure). Scope: the
generation pipeline — model, serving, safety — with the
text-to-image case assumed as the spatial foundation.

<Callout type="tip" title="Interview signal">
"Video = image generation + temporal consistency, and the cost is
dominated by the time dimension." Framing temporal coherence and
compute/storage as *the* problems — not per-frame quality — is the
differentiator.
</Callout>

## 2. Requirements & scale

| Dimension | Target | Consequence |
|---|---|---|
| Temporal consistency | Smooth, flicker-free, stable identities | Spatiotemporal attention; joint latent generation |
| Compute | Minutes of GPU per clip | Latent + spacetime compression; staged generation |
| Storage/bandwidth | Videos are huge | Aggressive latent compression; CDN delivery |
| Latency | Async (not interactive) | Job queue; progress streaming |

<ThinkFirst question="You already have a great text-to-image model. Why not just run it once per frame?">
Because each frame would be an **independent sample** from the image
distribution — and independence is exactly what video cannot tolerate.
Nothing ties frame two's dog to frame one's dog, so identity drifts;
nothing ties adjacent textures, so everything flickers; nothing ties
camera position, so the viewpoint jumps. Temporal coherence is a
property of the *joint* distribution over frames, so frames must be
generated jointly — which is why video models denoise a whole
spacetime latent with temporal attention, rather than looping an
image model however good it is.
</ThinkFirst>

## 3. GenAI problem framing

The framing the ThinkFirst forces: **spatiotemporal latent
diffusion**. Encode the clip into a compressed **spacetime latent** —
compression applied along both space *and* time, since adjacent
frames are highly redundant — then run a diffusion transformer over
that latent with both spatial and temporal attention, so the entire
clip denoises as one object. Generation is diffusion over the whole
clip's latent, not over any frame.

The pre-generative baseline is the failed experiment from the intro —
frame-by-frame generation — and it's worth stating *as* the baseline
in an interview, because explaining precisely why it fails
(independent samples, no joint structure) is the cleanest
demonstration that you understand what the spatiotemporal machinery
is for.

## 4. Data & training *(spine: optimization)*

The data problem: large video–text corpora, where the *text* half is
the bottleneck — raw video rarely comes with captions describing its
content and motion, so **captioning videos with a VLM is itself a
subsystem**, and caption quality gates prompt adherence exactly as it
did for images. Curation adds video-specific chores: dedup,
resolution and frame-rate normalization, and safety filtering of the
training corpus.

The optimization problem is scale, plainly: spacetime latents make
long sequences, models are large, and training runs on big clusters
with the full distributed toolkit — data/tensor/pipeline parallelism,
gradient checkpointing. In this case study, "optimization" is less
about loss functions than about the engineering that makes the loss
computable at all — and it is where the money goes.

<ThinkFirst question="Full attention over every spacetime patch of a 10-second clip — feasible? Where does the cost go, and what do you factor?">
Attention is quadratic in token count, and video multiplies tokens by
the frame count — so full 3D attention over all spacetime patches is
prohibitive at any useful resolution. Two factorizations tame it,
attacking different dimensions. **Factorized attention**: alternate
spatial attention (within each frame) and temporal attention (across
time at each spatial location) — each pass is quadratic in a much
smaller token set. **Staged generation**: produce a low-resolution,
low-frame-rate clip first, then super-resolve spatially and
interpolate temporally — so the expensive joint modeling happens on a
small problem and cheap specialist models do the upscaling. The
principle: never pay full price in one pass.
</ThinkFirst>

## 5. Model & generation *(spine: hypothesis-space)*

The architecture assembles the ThinkFirst's answers: a **diffusion
transformer over spacetime latent patches** with **factorized
spatial/temporal attention** — the design that keeps joint generation
affordable. **Conditioning** enters via cross-attention on the text
embedding (and optionally a first frame, which anchors both content
and the visual identity everything else must stay consistent with),
with CFG for adherence. And generation runs as the **staged cascade**:
base clip at low res/FPS → spatial super-resolution → temporal
interpolation, each stage cheaper and simpler than the one job it
inherits.

The visualization shows the same denoising idea as images — but here
the model denoises a *spacetime* latent, which is what keeps motion
and identity coherent across frames: they were never generated
separately in the first place.

## 6. Serving & scaling

Serving is **asynchronous by physics**: a request is a multi-minute
GPU job, so the pattern is a job queue with progress streaming (and
partial previews), never an interactive call. Large models run
multi-GPU per clip; pools autoscale on queue depth. Then the
often-forgotten half: the *outputs* are heavy too — finished clips
land in object storage, transcode to delivery formats, and serve
through a CDN. Video is the one generative modality where storage and
bandwidth engineering is a first-class part of the serving design
rather than a footnote.

<ThinkFirst question="Every individual frame scores beautifully on FID. Is the video good?">
Not necessarily — and this is the evaluation version of the intro's
failed experiment. Per-frame metrics are computed on frames in
isolation, so they are *structurally blind to time*: a clip whose
frames are individually photorealistic can still flicker, drift, and
morph between them, and per-frame FID will never know. Video needs
**temporal metrics** — FVD (distributional realism over whole
clips), warp/flicker consistency, motion smoothness — and ultimately
human judgment, because "does it look real *over time*" is the thing
viewers actually perceive and the hardest thing to automate.
</ThinkFirst>

## 7. Evaluation *(spine: evaluation)*

The metric stack mirrors the two quality axes. **Per-frame quality**
keeps its image-world metrics (FID, aesthetic scores) as a floor.
**Temporal quality** gets its own instruments per the ThinkFirst:
FVD over clips, flicker/warp consistency, motion smoothness.
**Prompt adherence** is scored with CLIP-style video–text alignment.
And as everywhere in generative evaluation — but most of all here —
**human preference dominates**: temporal realism is precisely the
perceptual judgment automated metrics approximate worst, so
side-by-side human evaluation makes the ship calls, and collected
preference data feeds the improvement loop.

## 8. Safety & guardrails

Video inherits the image safety stack and raises its stakes, because
**video deepfakes are the most potent synthetic-media abuse there
is** — moving, talking footage of a real person carries an evidentiary
weight no still image matches. The controls are layered accordingly:
consent/likeness gating on prompts and reference images, output
classifiers for recognizable real individuals, and mandatory
provenance — visible marks plus C2PA metadata — on everything
generated. Content safety filters extend from single images to
temporal content (actions and events, not just depictions), and the
IP posture carries over from images: training-data provenance and
memorization monitoring.

## 9. Tradeoffs & alternatives

| Choice | Alternative | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Joint spacetime diffusion | Frame-by-frame image gen | Independent frames flicker and drift; only joint generation buys coherence |
| Spacetime latent compression | Pixel-space video | Pixels are astronomically expensive at video scale |
| Staged (low-res → SR → interpolate) | Direct full-res generation | Far cheaper for the same output quality |
| Factorized space/time attention | Full 3D attention | Quadratic cost over frames × patches is prohibitive |

## 10. Interviewer follow-ups

<Details summary="Frames look great individually but the video flickers. Why, and fix?">
The diagnosis: frames are being generated too independently —
temporal coupling is too weak somewhere in the stack. The fixes
strengthen the coupling: more/stronger temporal attention so frames
denoise jointly, conditioning later frames on earlier latent state,
and temporal-consistency terms in training. And fix the measurement
too: track FVD and flicker metrics alongside per-frame FID, because
this failure is invisible to per-frame scores — which is exactly how
it reaches production unnoticed.
</Details>

<Details summary="Generating full-res video directly is impossibly expensive. Alternatives?">
Cascade it: generate a low-resolution, low-FPS clip in the compressed
spacetime latent — where the expensive joint spatiotemporal modeling
happens on the smallest possible problem — then hand off to cheap
specialists: spatial super-resolution, then temporal interpolation.
Each stage does one job on inputs the previous stage made tractable.
This staging is the standard recipe for video (and for very-high-res
images) because it delivers near-direct quality at a fraction of the
compute.
</Details>

<Details summary="How do you prevent realistic deepfakes of real people?">
Layered prevention plus traceability, because at these stakes no
single mechanism is trusted alone: consent/likeness gating on prompts
and reference images (stopping the request), output classifiers for
recognizable real individuals (stopping the result), and mandatory
provenance — visible marks and C2PA metadata — on everything generated
(making whatever escapes attributable). Video is the modality where
this matters most: moving footage of a real person is the
highest-impact synthetic-media abuse there is.
</Details>

## Key takeaways

- Text-to-video is **spatiotemporal diffusion**: frames must be
  generated *jointly* — the frame-by-frame shortcut fails by
  construction, and temporal consistency, not per-frame quality, is
  the defining challenge.
- The costs are tamed by **never paying full price in one pass**:
  spacetime latent compression, factorized space/time attention, and
  staged low-res → super-resolve → interpolate generation.
- Evaluation must be able to *see time*: **FVD and flicker metrics
  alongside per-frame scores**, with human preference as the final
  judge of temporal realism.
- Serving is an **async GPU job with heavy outputs** (queues, CDN,
  transcoding), and safety runs at maximum stakes: **deepfake
  gating and provenance watermarking are mandatory**.
