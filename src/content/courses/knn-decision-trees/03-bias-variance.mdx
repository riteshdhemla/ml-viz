# The Bias–Variance Tradeoff

You've now met two models with an obvious "complexity dial": the $k$ in [K-Nearest Neighbors](/courses/knn-decision-trees/01-knn) and the depth of a [decision tree](/courses/knn-decision-trees/02-decision-trees). Turn the dial one way and the model ignores real structure; turn it the other way and it hallucinates structure that isn't there. This tension has a name (the **bias–variance tradeoff**) and it governs every model you will ever train. It is the theory underneath the loop's **evaluation** stage: it explains *why* a model that looks great on training data can fail on new data, and what the generalization gap you measure is made of.

## The decomposition

Expected test error splits into three additive pieces: $\text{Bias}^2 + \text{Variance} + \sigma^2$ (irreducible noise). Bias is how far the average prediction is from the truth; variance is how much it swings across different training sets. Total error is minimized somewhere in between — simpler models accept bias to cut variance, complex models do the reverse.

## Seeing it in k

For KNN, $k$ controls how much the prediction averages over:

| | $k = 1$ | $k = n$ |
|---|---|---|
| Prediction | copy the nearest point | average everyone |
| Bias | minimal | maximal: predicts the global mean |
| Variance | huge: one noisy neighbor flips the answer | tiny |
| Boundary | jagged islands around noise points | nearly flat |

Small $k$ = low bias, high variance. Large $k$ = high bias, low variance. The best $k$ (found by cross-validation) sits where the two curves cross.

<Callout type="tip" title="Diagnosing which problem you have">
  Compare training and validation error. **Both high** → bias problem: your model can't even fit the data it has seen — add capacity or features. **Training low, validation high** → variance problem: regularize, simplify, or get more data. More data fixes variance; it never fixes bias.
</Callout>

## Key takeaways

- Test error = bias² + variance + irreducible noise
- Small $k$ / deep trees → low bias, high variance; large $k$ / shallow trees → the reverse
- Diagnose by comparing training vs. validation error; the gap is variance, the floor is bias
- More data cures variance, never bias; ensembles cancel the variance of low-bias models
