Every detection and segmentation model has two parts: a **backbone** that converts raw pixels into rich feature representations, and one or more **heads** that consume those features to produce boxes, masks, or labels. Getting the backbone right — both the architecture and the initialization — often matters more than the choice of head.

## The Backbone's Role

A backbone is a deep CNN (or Vision Transformer) trained to extract hierarchically rich features from images. Detection and segmentation heads typically attach to **intermediate feature maps** at multiple scales (e.g., strides 8, 16, 32) rather than only the final layer, so that the head has access to both fine-grained detail and high-level semantics simultaneously.

**Pre-training on ImageNet** (1.28M images across 1,000 classes) provides a powerful weight initialization. Features learned for classifying cats, cars, and trees generalize broadly: early layers detect edges and textures; middle layers detect parts; late layers encode object-level semantics. Fine-tuning from this initialization converges faster and reaches higher accuracy than training from scratch — especially when downstream labeled data is limited.

## ResNet — Residual Connections

Very deep networks (50+ layers) suffer from vanishing gradients: by the time the loss signal propagates back through dozens of layers, it shrinks to near zero and early layers stop learning.

**Residual connections** solve this by adding a shortcut path that bypasses one or more layers:

$$\mathbf{y} = F(\mathbf{x}, \{W_i\}) + \mathbf{x}$$

where $F(\mathbf{x})$ is the residual mapping learned by the stacked convolutions. During backpropagation, gradients flow **directly through the identity shortcut** without passing through any weights — the gradient of $\mathbf{y}$ with respect to $\mathbf{x}$ has an additive term of $1$ regardless of $F$'s weights.

### Bottleneck Block

ResNet-50 and deeper variants use a three-layer bottleneck block to keep compute manageable:

1. $1 \times 1$ conv — reduce channels (e.g., $256 \to 64$)
2. $3 \times 3$ conv — spatial feature extraction at reduced channel count
3. $1 \times 1$ conv — expand channels back (e.g., $64 \to 256$)

The $3 \times 3$ convolution operates on only 64 channels instead of 256, cutting compute by $\approx 4\times$ compared to a plain two-layer block.

### ResNet Variants

| Model | Stages (blocks) | Params | GFLOPs |
|-------|----------------|--------|--------|
| ResNet-50 | 3–4–6–3 | 25M | 4.1 |
| ResNet-101 | 3–4–23–3 | 44M | 7.8 |
| ResNet-152 | 3–8–36–3 | 60M | 11.6 |

ResNet-50 with a Feature Pyramid Network (FPN) head is the most common baseline backbone for detection and segmentation benchmarks.

## MobileNet — Depthwise Separable Convolutions

A standard $K \times K$ convolution over $C_{in}$ input channels producing $C_{out}$ output channels requires:

$$K \times K \times C_{in} \times C_{out} \text{ multiplications per output location}$$

**Depthwise separable convolution** splits this into two steps:

1. **Depthwise conv** — one $K \times K$ filter per input channel (no cross-channel mixing): $K \times K \times C_{in}$ multiplications
2. **Pointwise conv** — $1 \times 1 \times C_{in} \times C_{out}$ (cross-channel mixing, no spatial): $C_{in} \times C_{out}$ multiplications

**Cost reduction factor:**

$$\frac{K^2 C_{in} + C_{in} C_{out}}{K^2 C_{in} C_{out}} = \frac{1}{C_{out}} + \frac{1}{K^2}$$

For $K = 3$ and $C_{out} = 256$: $\frac{1}{256} + \frac{1}{9} \approx 0.115$, meaning **8–9× cheaper** than a standard convolution with minimal accuracy loss.

### MobileNet Variants

**MobileNetV2 — Inverted Residuals.** Counterintuitively, residual shortcuts connect *narrow* (low-channel) layers, while the $3 \times 3$ depthwise conv operates in an *expanded* (high-channel) intermediate space. This prevents information loss in the bottleneck while keeping the shortcut cheap.

**MobileNetV3 — SE Attention + Hard-Swish.** Adds Squeeze-and-Excite (SE) blocks that reweight channels by global context, and replaces swish activation with hard-swish ($\frac{x \cdot \text{ReLU6}(x+3)}{6}$) — equivalent performance but integer-arithmetic friendly for mobile hardware.

## EfficientNet — Compound Scaling

Prior approaches scaled networks along a single axis: make it deeper (more layers), wider (more channels), or feed higher-resolution inputs. EfficientNet's insight is that **all three axes should be scaled jointly** — depth, width, and resolution are interdependent.

### Compound Scaling Rule

A single compound coefficient $\phi$ controls total compute:

$$\text{depth} = 1.2^\phi, \quad \text{width} = 1.1^\phi, \quad \text{resolution} = 1.15^\phi$$

Total FLOPs scale as approximately $2^\phi$. The base ratios $(1.2,\ 1.1,\ 1.15)$ were found by neural architecture search (NAS) on a constrained grid.

### EfficientNet-B0 to B7

| Model | $\phi$ | Input | Params | Top-1 |
|-------|--------|-------|--------|-------|
| B0 | 0 | 224 | 5.3M | 77.1% |
| B4 | 4 | 380 | 19M | 82.6% |
| B7 | 7 | 600 | 66M | 84.3% |

EfficientNet-B0 was found by NAS to be the optimal baseline architecture for the given FLOP budget; B1–B7 scale it up using the compound rule. EfficientNet-B4 is a popular backbone choice when accuracy matters and compute is moderate.

## Transfer Learning in Practice

### Two Regimes

**Feature extraction** — freeze all backbone weights; train only the task-specific head. Use when:
- Downstream dataset is small (hundreds to low thousands of images)
- Downstream task is similar to ImageNet classification

**Full fine-tuning** — unfreeze the entire network; train end-to-end with a low learning rate for the backbone. Use when:
- Downstream dataset is large enough to support it
- Downstream domain differs significantly from ImageNet (medical imaging, satellite imagery)

### Layer-wise Learning Rate Decay

Earlier backbone layers learn general features (Gabor-like edges, color blobs) that generalize across tasks — they should change very little. Later layers encode task-specific semantics and can change more.

A common schedule: multiply the learning rate by a decay factor $\alpha < 1$ for each earlier layer group. For example with $\alpha = 0.1$ and a head LR of $10^{-3}$:

- Head: $10^{-3}$
- Final backbone stage: $2 \times 10^{-4}$
- Earlier stages: $10^{-4}$, $2 \times 10^{-5}$, …

This prevents destroying the general-purpose ImageNet features while still allowing fine-tuning for the new task.

## ViT as Backbone

Vision Transformers (ViT) treat an image as a sequence of non-overlapping $16 \times 16$ patches. Each patch is flattened, projected to an embedding dimension, and processed by standard Transformer self-attention blocks.

- **ViT-B/16** — 86M parameters, 17.6 GFLOPs per 224×224 image
- Pre-trained on ImageNet-21k (14M images), then fine-tuned on ImageNet-1k, ViT-B/16 achieves 81.8% top-1 — competitive with ResNet-50 at 4× the compute
- **DeiT** (Data-efficient Image Transformers) adds a distillation token to train ViT from scratch on ImageNet-1k without extra data, reaching 79.8% at ResNet-50 compute

ViT backbones excel when pre-training data is large. For smaller datasets or tighter compute budgets, ResNet and EfficientNet remain preferred.

## Choosing a Backbone

| Backbone | Params | GFLOPs | ImageNet Top-1 | Use case |
|----------|--------|--------|----------------|---------|
| ResNet-50 | 25M | 4.1 | 76% | General baseline |
| MobileNetV3-L | 5.4M | 0.22 | 75% | Mobile / edge deployment |
| EfficientNet-B4 | 19M | 4.2 | 82.6% | Accuracy/speed balance |
| ViT-B/16 | 86M | 17.6 | 81.8% | Large pre-training datasets |

Rules of thumb:
- **Prototype or resource-constrained**: MobileNetV3
- **Standard benchmark baseline**: ResNet-50 + FPN
- **High-accuracy production model**: EfficientNet-B4 or B5
- **Abundant compute and data**: ViT-B/16 or larger

<Callout type="tip">
**Always normalize inputs to ImageNet statistics** when using any pre-trained backbone. The weights were trained expecting pixel values normalized to mean $\mu = [0.485,\ 0.456,\ 0.406]$ and standard deviation $\sigma = [0.229,\ 0.224,\ 0.225]$ (per channel, RGB). Skipping normalization produces activations outside the trained distribution and severely degrades transfer performance — even when fine-tuning end-to-end.
</Callout>

## Common Mistakes

**Using ResNet-50 when MobileNet suffices.** On simple classification tasks or edge deployment, ResNet-50 uses 19× more FLOPs than MobileNetV3-L for roughly the same accuracy. Always profile before defaulting to ResNet.

**Fine-tuning with too high a learning rate.** A learning rate suitable for training from scratch will destroy the learned ImageNet features in the first few gradient steps. Use $10^{-4}$ or lower for the backbone, with layer-wise decay toward early layers.

**Not normalizing inputs to ImageNet mean/std.** Pre-trained weights are tightly coupled to the statistics of their training data. Even after fine-tuning, starting with wrong normalization causes slow convergence and lower final accuracy.

**Attaching heads only to the final backbone feature map.** Heads that consume only stride-32 features lack the spatial resolution needed for small objects or fine segmentation boundaries. Use intermediate features (stride 8, 16, 32) via FPN or similar multi-scale structures.

---

## Key Takeaways

- Backbones extract hierarchical features from raw images; detection and segmentation heads attach to intermediate multi-scale feature maps.
- ResNet's identity shortcut $F(\mathbf{x}) + \mathbf{x}$ ensures gradient flow through very deep networks by adding an additive $1$ term in the chain rule.
- Depthwise separable convolutions reduce compute by a factor of $1/C_{out} + 1/K^2$ — roughly 8–9× for $K=3$ — enabling efficient mobile architectures.
- EfficientNet scales depth, width, and resolution jointly with a compound coefficient $\phi$, achieving better accuracy per FLOP than single-axis scaling.
- Transfer learning from ImageNet is almost always worth it; use feature extraction for small datasets and full fine-tuning (with low LR and layer-wise decay) for larger ones.
- Always normalize inputs to ImageNet mean and standard deviation; never fine-tune a pre-trained backbone with the same learning rate as the head.
