# SVD & Low-Rank Approximation

[Eigendecomposition](/courses/linear-algebra/03-eigenvalues-and-eigenvectors) was powerful but picky: it needs a square matrix, and the nicest guarantees only arrive when the matrix is symmetric. The **singular value decomposition** drops every restriction. *Any* matrix — rectangular, rank-deficient, ugly — factors as:

$$
A = U \Sigma V^\top
$$

- $V^\top$ — an orthogonal **rotation** of the input space (columns of $V$ = right singular vectors)
- $\Sigma$ — a diagonal **stretch** by the singular values $\sigma_1 \ge \sigma_2 \ge \cdots \ge 0$
- $U$ — an orthogonal **rotation** of the output space (columns = left singular vectors)

Every linear map, no matter how messy, is just *rotate → stretch along perpendicular axes → rotate*. The singular values are the stretch factors, and they are always real and non-negative.

## Where it comes from

The matrices $A^\top A$ and $A A^\top$ are always symmetric, so the [spectral theorem](/courses/linear-algebra/03-eigenvalues-and-eigenvectors) — which guarantees symmetric matrices always have real eigenvalues and mutually orthogonal eigenvectors — applies to them even when it doesn't apply to $A$:

- $V$ = eigenvectors of $A^\top A$
- $U$ = eigenvectors of $A A^\top$
- $\sigma_i = \sqrt{\lambda_i}$, the square roots of their (shared, non-negative) eigenvalues

This is also exactly the [PCA](/courses/pca-dimensionality/01-pca) connection: for centered data $X$, the covariance is $\frac{1}{n}X^\top X$, so the principal components *are* the right singular vectors of $X$, and explained variances are $\sigma_i^2 / n$. Numerical libraries compute PCA via SVD rather than forming the covariance matrix — squaring the data into $X^\top X$ doubles the condition number and loses precision, so the SVD of $X$ is preferred ([Golub & Van Loan, *Matrix Computations*](https://www.hfsbooks.com/books/matrix-computations-4th-edition-golub-van-loan/)).

## The sum-of-layers view and best rank-$k$ approximation

Rewriting $A = U\Sigma V^\top$ one column at a time gives a sum of rank-1 layers: $A = \sum_i \sigma_i \mathbf{u}_i\mathbf{v}_i^\top$. Truncating to the first $k$ layers gives $A_k$ — and the **Eckart–Young theorem** proves this is the best rank-$k$ approximation in Frobenius norm, with error $\sqrt{\sum_{i>k}\sigma_i^2}$ ([Eckart & Young, 1936](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF02288367)).

**Quick numeric example.** Suppose $A$ has singular values $[10, 5, 0.3]$. The full Frobenius norm is $\sqrt{10^2+5^2+0.3^2} = \sqrt{125.09} \approx 11.18$. Keeping only $k=2$ gives error $\sqrt{0.3^2} = 0.3$ — we captured 99.9% of the "signal" in just two layers. Keeping only $k=1$ gives error $\sqrt{5^2+0.3^2} = \sqrt{25.09} \approx 5.01$ — still meaningful structure, but a lot is lost. The singular value spectrum tells you at a glance where to cut.

This fact powers image compression, recommender systems, [LoRA fine-tuning](https://arxiv.org/abs/2106.09685) — which adapts a large model by learning a low-rank update to its weight matrices ([Hu et al., 2021](https://arxiv.org/abs/2106.09685)) — and noise filtering.

<WikiLink slug="svd-low-rank" title="SVD and the Eckart-Young Theorem" />

```python
import numpy as np

rng = np.random.default_rng(0)
A = rng.normal(size=(60, 40)) @ rng.normal(size=(40, 40))

U, s, Vt = np.linalg.svd(A, full_matrices=False)

k = 10
A_k = U[:, :k] @ np.diag(s[:k]) @ Vt[:k, :]
err = np.linalg.norm(A - A_k, "fro")
tail = np.sqrt((s[k:] ** 2).sum())
print(f"rank-{k} error = {err:.4f}, tail = {tail:.4f}")  # identical
```

Sweep the truncation rank $k$ below and watch the rank-$k$ reconstruction sharpen as the discarded singular values — and the Frobenius error $\sqrt{\sum_{i>k}\sigma_i^2}$ — shrink. A few layers capture almost all the signal.

## Key takeaways

- SVD factors **any** matrix into rotation · stretch · rotation; singular values are the stretch factors, always real and ≥ 0
- $U$, $V$ are eigenvectors of $AA^\top$ and $A^\top A$; $\sigma_i = \sqrt{\lambda_i}$ — and PCA is the SVD of centered data
- $A = \sum_i \sigma_i \mathbf{u}_i \mathbf{v}_i^\top$: a stack of rank-1 layers, sorted by importance
- Truncating the stack gives the provably best rank-k approximation (Eckart–Young) — the engine behind compression, recommenders, denoising, and LoRA

## Further reading

- [Matrix Computations](https://www.hfsbooks.com/books/matrix-computations-4th-edition-golub-van-loan/) — Golub & Van Loan; how the SVD is actually computed, and why PCA-via-SVD is more stable.
