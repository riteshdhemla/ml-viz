# Transfer Learning

Training a CNN from scratch requires millions of images and days of computation. Transfer learning lets you **reuse a pretrained network** and adapt it to your task with much less data.

**Real-world examples:** Medical imaging (only hundreds of X-rays available), satellite imagery classification, industrial defect detection, rare species identification.

## The key insight

A CNN trained on ImageNet (1.2M images, 1000 classes) has learned:
- **Early layers:** universal features — edges, textures, colors
- **Middle layers:** object parts — eyes, wheels, leaves
- **Late layers:** task-specific features — dog breeds, car models

The early and middle layers are **universal** — they work for almost any image task. Only the late layers need adaptation.

<Callout type="tip" title="Intuition">
  It's like learning to paint. First you learn to see shapes, colors, and light (universal skills). Then you specialize — portraits, landscapes, abstract art. Transfer learning skips the "learning to see" part.
</Callout>

### Why freezing early layers works — the gradient view

"Freezing" a layer means setting `requires_grad = False` on its parameters. Concretely, during backprop the optimizer simply **skips the weight update** for that layer:

$$
W \leftarrow W - \eta \,\frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial W}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
W \leftarrow W \quad (\text{no change, gradient discarded})
$$

This is justified by two facts:

1. **Early-layer features are already near-optimal.** Gabor-like edge and color detectors emerge in the first conv layers of *almost every* vision model regardless of the final task. Their gradient $\partial \mathcal{L}/\partial W$ on your small dataset is mostly noise — updating them risks **overfitting** more than it helps.

2. **Frozen layers cost nothing to "train."** Their gradients are never computed, so each step is cheaper, and the optimizer has far fewer parameters to estimate — exactly what you want when data is scarce. With $N$ trainable parameters you need roughly $\mathcal{O}(N)$ examples to constrain them; freezing slashes $N$ by orders of magnitude (we count this exactly below).

The forward pass still flows through the frozen layers — they act as a **fixed feature extractor** turning your image into a 2048-dimensional embedding (for ResNet-50). Only the head learns to map that embedding to your classes.

## Three strategies

### Strategy 1: Feature extraction (least data)

**Freeze** all pretrained layers, replace only the final classifier:

```python
import torch.nn as nn
from torchvision import models

# Load pretrained ResNet
model = models.resnet50(weights='IMAGENET1K_V2')

# Freeze all layers
for param in model.parameters():
    param.requires_grad = False

# Replace classifier (only this part trains)
model.fc = nn.Sequential(
    nn.Linear(2048, 256),
    nn.ReLU(),
    nn.Dropout(0.3),
    nn.Linear(256, num_classes)
)

# Count what's actually trainable
trainable = sum(p.numel() for p in model.parameters() if p.requires_grad)
total = sum(p.numel() for p in model.parameters())
print(f"Trainable: {trainable:,} / {total:,} ({100*trainable/total:.1f}%)")
```

**When to use:** < 1,000 training images. The pretrained features are already good — you just need a simple classifier.

#### Worked parameter count — swapping the head on ResNet-50

ResNet-50 ships with **25,557,032** parameters and an ImageNet head `fc = Linear(2048, 1000)`. A fully-connected layer with $d_\text{in}$ inputs and $d_\text{out}$ outputs has

$$
\#\text{params} = \underbrace{d_\text{in}\cdot d_\text{out}}_{\text{weights}} + \underbrace{d_\text{out}}_{\text{biases}}.
$$

**Original head** ($2048 \to 1000$):

$$
2048 \times 1000 + 1000 = 2{,}049{,}000 \text{ params.}
$$

Strip it off and the **frozen backbone** keeps the rest:

$$
25{,}557{,}032 - 2{,}049{,}000 = 23{,}508{,}032 \text{ frozen params.}
$$

Now bolt on the new two-layer head above for, say, $k = 2$ classes (pneumonia vs normal):

$$
\begin{aligned}
\text{Linear}(2048 \to 256): &\quad 2048 \times 256 + 256 = 524{,}544 \\
\text{ReLU, Dropout}: &\quad 0 \quad (\text{no learnable params}) \\
\text{Linear}(256 \to 2): &\quad 256 \times 2 + 2 = 514 \\
\hline
\text{new head total}: &\quad 525{,}058
\end{aligned}
$$

So the model now has $23{,}508{,}032 + 525{,}058 = 24{,}033{,}090$ parameters, but only the head is trainable:

$$
\frac{525{,}058}{24{,}033{,}090} \approx \boxed{2.2\%}
$$

**Only 2.2% of the network learns.** If you instead used a plain `Linear(2048, 2)` head ($4{,}098$ params) the trainable fraction drops to a stunning **0.017%** — you are fitting ~4K numbers, which a few hundred images can comfortably constrain. *This* is why feature extraction works on tiny datasets.

### Strategy 2: Fine-tuning (medium data)

Unfreeze the last few layers and train with a **small learning rate**:

```python
# Unfreeze last 2 blocks
for param in model.layer4.parameters():
    param.requires_grad = True

# Use different learning rates
optimizer = torch.optim.Adam([
    {'params': model.layer4.parameters(), 'lr': 1e-4},   # small LR
    {'params': model.fc.parameters(), 'lr': 1e-3}         # normal LR
])
```

**When to use:** 1,000-100,000 images. The task is somewhat different from ImageNet (e.g., medical images vs natural images).

#### "Last few layers" is not a few parameters

It's tempting to think unfreezing `layer4` only adds a little. In ResNet-50, `layer4` is the **widest stage** (it works with up to 2048 channels), so it alone holds about **14.96M** parameters — roughly **58.6%** of the whole network:

$$
\frac{14{,}964{,}736}{25{,}557{,}032} \approx 58.6\%.
$$

Unfreezing `layer4` plus the new head from Strategy 1 makes

$$
14{,}964{,}736 + 525{,}058 = 15{,}489{,}794
$$

parameters trainable — about **64%** of the model. So "fine-tune the last block" is really "retrain the majority of the weights." That is exactly why you need more data (1K–100K) and a smaller learning rate than feature extraction.

#### Why discriminative (layer-wise) learning rates

The two parameter groups above use **different** learning rates on purpose:

- The **new head** is randomly initialized — its weights are *garbage*, so it needs a normal-sized LR ($\approx 10^{-3}$) to learn fast.
- The **pretrained `layer4`** is already excellent — it only needs gentle nudging, so it gets a $10\times$ smaller LR ($\approx 10^{-4}$).

If you used the head's large LR on the backbone, the first few mini-batches — when the head's gradients are huge and meaningless — would flow back and **overwrite the pretrained features** before the head has learned anything useful. This is *catastrophic forgetting*. Discriminative LRs (often a geometric decay like $\eta_\ell = \eta_{\text{head}} \cdot \gamma^{\,(L-\ell)}$ for layer $\ell$, with $\gamma \approx 0.3$–$0.5$) keep early layers nearly fixed while letting the head move freely.

A common companion trick is to **warm up the head first**: freeze the backbone for a few epochs (feature extraction) until the head's gradients are sane, *then* unfreeze `layer4` for fine-tuning.

### Strategy 3: Full fine-tuning (lots of data)

Unfreeze everything and train with a very small learning rate:

```python
# Unfreeze everything
for param in model.parameters():
    param.requires_grad = True

# Very small learning rate to avoid destroying pretrained features
optimizer = torch.optim.Adam(model.parameters(), lr=1e-5)
```

**When to use:** > 100,000 images. The task is very different from ImageNet (e.g., satellite imagery, medical scans).

## Decision guide

The choice hinges on two axes: **how much labeled data you have** (more data lets you safely train more parameters without overfitting) and **how different your images are from ImageNet** (a bigger domain gap means the late, task-specific features transfer poorly and must be relearned).

| Data Size | Task Similarity | Strategy |
|-----------|----------------|----------|
| < 1K | Similar to ImageNet | Feature extraction |
| < 1K | Different from ImageNet | Feature extraction + augmentation |
| 1K-100K | Similar | Fine-tune last layers |
| 1K-100K | Different | Fine-tune more layers |
| > 100K | Any | Full fine-tuning |

The logic of the table is a simple guardrail: **trainable parameters $\lesssim$ examples.** With ~1K images you can responsibly fit the ~4K–525K parameters of a new head (feature extraction), but not the ~15M of `layer4` — you'd overfit. As data grows you can afford to unfreeze more, and a large domain gap *forces* you to (because the frozen features no longer match your images).

<Callout type="warning" title="Learning rate is critical">
  When fine-tuning, use a learning rate 10-100× smaller than training from scratch. The pretrained weights sit in a good local minimum; a from-scratch LR produces large updates whose first steps move the weights *out* of that minimum faster than the loss can pull them back — you destroy in one epoch what ImageNet spent days learning. Pair the small LR with a scheduler (cosine or step decay) so updates shrink further as you approach the new optimum.
</Callout>

## Practical tips

1. **Start with feature extraction** — It's fast and often sufficient. Only move to fine-tuning if accuracy is insufficient.

2. **Use data augmentation** — Transfer learning shines with small datasets. Augment aggressively:
```python
from torchvision import transforms

transform = transforms.Compose([
    transforms.RandomHorizontalFlip(),
    transforms.RandomRotation(15),
    transforms.ColorJitter(brightness=0.2, contrast=0.2),
    transforms.RandomAffine(degrees=0, translate=(0.1, 0.1)),
    transforms.ToTensor(),
    transforms.Normalize([0.485, 0.456, 0.406], [0.229, 0.224, 0.225])
])
```

3. **Use the right pretrained model:**
   - **ResNet-50:** Good default, balanced speed/accuracy
   - **EfficientNet-B0:** Best accuracy per parameter
   - **MobileNet:** Mobile/edge deployment
   - **ViT:** State-of-the-art but needs more data

4. **Monitor overfitting** — With small datasets, overfitting is the #1 enemy. Use early stopping, dropout, and weight decay.

## Worked example: Medical image classification

Task: Classify X-rays as pneumonia vs normal (1,000 training images).

```python
import torch
from torchvision import models, transforms
from torch.utils.data import DataLoader

# 1. Load pretrained model
model = models.resnet50(weights='IMAGENET1K_V2')

# 2. Freeze early layers
for param in model.parameters():
    param.requires_grad = False

# 3. Replace classifier with a 2-class head
model.fc = nn.Linear(2048, 2)   # 2048*2 + 2 = 4,098 trainable params

# 4. Train with appropriate settings
optimizer = torch.optim.Adam(model.fc.parameters(), lr=1e-3)
criterion = nn.CrossEntropyLoss()

# Of ResNet-50's ~25.5M params, only 4,098 (0.017%) update here.
# With only 1K images, feature extraction typically achieves 85-90% accuracy
# Fine-tuning the last block might push it to 90-93%
# Training from scratch with 1K images: likely < 70%
```

With 1,000 images and a 4,098-parameter head you are fitting only ~4 parameters per training image — comfortably constrained. Training all 25.5M parameters from scratch on the same data means ~25,557 parameters per image, vastly underdetermined, which is why it overfits to near-uselessness on this dataset.

## Common mistakes

1. **Using ImageNet normalization on non-RGB data** — Medical images, grayscale, or satellite images need different normalization stats.
2. **Fine-tuning too aggressively** — Large learning rates destroy pretrained features. Start small.
3. **Ignoring domain mismatch** — If your images look very different from ImageNet (e.g., X-rays, aerial photos), the pretrained features may not transfer well. Consider training from scratch if you have enough data.

## Key takeaways

- **Transfer learning** reuses features learned on a huge dataset (ImageNet) for your task.
- **Freezing** a layer discards its gradient so its weights never update — the backbone becomes a fixed feature extractor and only the head learns.
- A swapped head is tiny: ResNet-50's $2048\to1000$ ImageNet head ($2{,}049{,}000$ params) becomes a $2048\to2$ head ($4{,}098$ params), and feature extraction trains as little as **0.017%** of the network.
- "Fine-tune the last block" is misleadingly small: ResNet-50's `layer4` is ~15M params (**58.6%** of the model), so unfreezing it retrains the majority of the weights — hence the need for more data and a smaller LR.
- Use **discriminative learning rates** (small for the pretrained backbone, larger for the new head) and a from-scratch-÷10–100 LR to avoid catastrophic forgetting.
- Little data → freeze the backbone, train a new head; lots → fine-tune more layers.
